## 1 t-tests

All three tests compute `t = (estimate - hypothesized value) / standard error`.

| Test | Estimate | Standard error | Degrees of freedom |
|---|---|---|---|
| One sample | mean of the column | `SD / sqrt(n)` | `n - 1` |
| Two sample, equal variance (Origin's default) | `mean1 - mean2` | pooled: `sqrt(s_p^2 (1/n1 + 1/n2))` | `n1 + n2 - 2` |
| Two sample, unequal variance (Welch) | `mean1 - mean2` | `sqrt(s1^2/n1 + s2^2/n2)` | Satterthwaite |
| Paired | mean of the differences of the rows where both columns have a number | `SD(d) / sqrt(n)` | `n - 1` |

Options and defaults in Origin: the hypothesized mean (or difference of means) is 0, the
significance level is 0.05, and the alternative is two-tailed. The upper tail alternative is "the
estimate is larger than the hypothesized value" (`p = P(T > t)`), the lower tail "smaller" (`p = P(T < t)`),
the two-sided p-value is `2 P(T > |t|)`. The null hypothesis is rejected when `p < alpha`.

Confidence interval: it belongs to the estimate (the mean, the difference of the means, the mean
difference), not to the test statistic. A two-tailed test gives the interval of level `1 - alpha`; a
one-tailed test gives the interval of level `1 - 2 alpha`, so that its one finite end is the one-sided
bound of level `1 - alpha` (for alpha 0.05 and an upper-tail test the 90 % interval is reported).
Origin's interval for the unequal-variance test is the pooled one (pooled standard error, `n1 + n2 - 2`
degrees of freedom), while its t, DF and Prob are Welch's. Sheaf does the same (measured: on
A = 10 + 2 sin(3i) + i/4, i = 1..8, and B = 11.5 + 2.5 cos(2i) + i/5, i = 1..10, Origin gives DF 15.634 and
LCL -3.34117967, UCL 0.17642427, the pooled interval to 8 digits; test
`welch_test_reports_the_pooled_interval_as_origin` in `crates/calc/src/hypothesis.rs`).

Sheaf reports N, mean, SD and SEM of each sample, the estimate, its standard error, the hypothesized
value, t, DF, Prob, the confidence level, LCL, UCL and a 0/1 Reject column, and one plain-English line
per test.

## 2 One-way ANOVA

Each column is a group (missing values are ignored). The summary table has the rows Model (between
groups), Error (within) and Total with DF, sum of squares and mean square, and for Model the
statistic F = MS(model) / MS(error) and `Prob>F` from the F distribution. Origin also reports the
descriptive statistics of the groups (N, mean, SD, SEM) and R-squared (`SS model / SS total`), the root of
the error mean square and its ratio to the grand mean ("COV"). Sheaf writes the same into one sheet:
a groups table, the ANOVA table, a small table of R-squared, root MSE, COV and grand mean, and the
comparison table below.

Means comparison of all pairs (Tukey is Origin's usual choice and our default). For groups `i < j`:
difference `mean_j - mean_i`, standard error `sqrt(MSE (1/n_i + 1/n_j))`, with `m` the number of pairs and
`t` the Student t with the error degrees of freedom:

| Method | Statistic | p-value | Interval half width |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tukey | q = `|diff| sqrt(2) / se` (studentized range) | `P(Q > q)` for k groups | `q_crit se / sqrt 2` |
| Bonferroni | `|t|` | `min(1, m p_t)` | `t_(alpha/m) se` |
| Sidak | `|t|` | `1 - (1 - p_t)^m` | `t` at `1 - (1-alpha)^(1/m)` |
| Fisher LSD | `|t|` | `p_t`, no adjustment | `t_alpha se` |
| Scheffe | F = `diff^2 / (se^2 (k-1))` | F distribution, k-1 and error DF | `sqrt((k-1) F_crit) se` |

The pairs come in Origin's order, column by column: (1,2), (1,3), (2,3), (1,4), (2,4), (3,4), ... (a run with
four groups showed this; the first version listed them row by row, which is the same for three groups).
A pair is marked significant when its p-value is below alpha. The studentized range distribution
(`ptukey`, `qtukey`) is our own implementation of the published algorithm; statrs has none. Two groups
give the same p-value as the pooled two-sample t-test (checked).

## 3 Frequency counts and histogram

`freqcounts` counts how many values fall into each bin. What black-box runs showed:

- A bin holds its lower edge and not its upper edge: `[lo, hi)`. A value exactly on the last edge of the
  range is in no bin (so counts can sum to less than N). With the number of bins given and no end, the
  largest value is included.
- The number of bins is `(max - min) / size`, rounded to the nearest whole number (10/3 gives 3 bins,
  7/2 gives 4).
- Outlier rows (values below the first edge and at or above the last) are optional. In one case Origin
  moves the largest value from the row above into the row below when both rows are on; that looks like a
  fault in Origin and is not copied.
- Bin centre is the middle of the bin; bin end is the upper edge. The result columns are bin centre or
  end, count, cumulative count, and percent.
- Automatic range: when the range and size are left on automatic, the first edge is a multiple of the
  bin size below the smallest value and the last edge a multiple above the largest, with a margin of
  about a tenth of a bin: a data value that comes within that margin of an edge makes the range one bin
  wider on that side. The margin is not a clean threshold in Origin (a gap of 0.0802 bins did not widen
  the end for one sample, 0.0855 did for another); ours is 0.1 at the start and 0.08 at the end, which
  reproduces 98 % of 600 random starts and ends.
- Automatic bin size (measured 2026-11-13 on 600 generated cases of Origin's `RoundBinningRange(min, max, n)`,
  which depends on n, min and max only; `tools/origin-ref/stats-bins2.lines.txt`, fixture
  `crates/calc/tests/fixtures/bins_origin.rs`): the number of bins is `m = round(1.75 ln n)` (at least 2;
  steps at n = 5, 8, 14, 24, 42), the size is `range / (m - 0.19)` rounded to the nearest round number:
  one significant digit for n < 14 (sizes such as 0.9, 3, 9, 40000 occur), and 1, 2 or 5 times a power of
  ten from n = 14 (midpoints 1.5, 3.5, 7.5). Sheaf reproduces 593 of 600 sizes (the misses lie within
  rounding of a midpoint; the offset 0.19 is a fit, 0.2 gives 9 misses). The first and last edge follow
  from the size as described above, with 28 differences in 566 cases; in a further 34 cases Origin returns a
  last edge that is not a multiple of the size (for example -1.6212428 for data ending at -1.622), not copied.
  When it matters the user gives the bin size or number of bins, which agree exactly. For 40 values with a
  range of about 9.4 Origin chose 5 bins of 2; for 8 values between 9.9 and 13.4 bins of 0.9.

The histogram tool in Sheaf writes the counts to a sheet (bin centre as X, bin end, count, cumulative
count, percent) and, unless switched off, makes a graph with a column plot (`PlotKind::Column`) of the
counts against the bin centres, with the count axis starting at zero. The bars are drawn by the existing
column plot (width 0.7 of the smallest centre spacing), so there is a gap between the bars; Origin's
histogram has none. Closing that gap needs a change in `crates/plot` and is not done.

## 4 Normality tests

Origin offers Shapiro-Wilk (`swtest`), Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Lilliefors, Anderson-Darling and
D'Agostino-Pearson; Sheaf computes all five for each column and reports the statistic and p-value of each.

| Test | Needs | Statistic | Agreement with Origin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shapiro-Wilk | 3 to 5000 numbers | W | W to 1e-9, p (Royston's approximation) to 1e-6 |
| Kolmogorov-Smirnov | 2 numbers | D against a normal with the sample mean and SD | D exact; p is the exact distribution for given parameters. 70 further Origin runs (n = 5 to 100, `ks-probe.lines.txt`) show: Origin agrees with the exact p within 3 % wherever p < 0.5; above that its p is higher than the exact one (0.97 against 0.86 for n = 40) and is exactly 1 for a scaled statistic `(sqrt(n) + 0.12 + 0.11/sqrt(n)) D` below about 0.57. It is not a single function of that scaled statistic and none of the Kolmogorov series (1 to 100 terms, Stephens' or plain scaling) reproduced it, so the rule is not copied |
| Lilliefors | 5 numbers | same D | p below 0.1 to six digits (Dallal and Wilkinson); above 0.1 Origin interpolates in a table we do not have, ours is within 8 % (checked on 70 more runs, n = 5 to 100); capped at 0.2 as in Origin |
| Anderson-Darling | 8 numbers | A squared | A squared to 1e-9, p to 1e-6 |
| D'Agostino-Pearson | 8 numbers (kurtosis part 5) | K squared with 2 DF | z-scores, K squared and p to 1e-6 |

Normality is rejected when the p-value is below alpha (0.05 by default). Constant data and samples
that are too small give no value for a test (shown as "--" and named in the sentence).

## 7 Two-way ANOVA

Origin: Statistics > ANOVA > Two-Way ANOVA. It is an operation (no X-Function can be run from LabTalk), so
the reference runs call the library functions behind it, `stats_anova_two_way_compute_anova_table` and
`stats_anova_two_way_means_comparison` (`stats2-ref.c`, `stats2-anova2.lines.txt`). The library's own help says
the table comes from linear regressions on dummy variables, which is what lets cells have different sizes
(empty cells are not supported).

**Input.** Origin takes indexed data (a column with the level of factor A of each row, one for factor B and
one with the data) or raw data (every column is one cell of the design; the levels of A and B are set per
column). It needs at least 4 numbers and at least two levels in each factor; rows with a missing number are
dropped. Levels are numbered in the order they first appear (indexed) or in the order of the columns (raw).
Sheaf takes both: `Tool::TwoWayAnova` with `TwoWayLayout::Indexed` (columns A, B, data; the factor columns may
hold text or numbers) or `TwoWayLayout::Raw` (the levels per data column in the spec); command line
`--y A,B,DATA` or `--raw --y COLS --levels-a ... --levels-b ...`.

**Table.** Rows A, B, A*B (only with the interaction switched on, which Sheaf does by default), Model, Error
and Total, with DF, sum of squares, mean square, F and Prob>F for every row that has a test.

**Which sums of squares (measured).** The sum of squares of a term is the rise of the residual sum of squares
when the term is taken out of the full model, with every factor coded by sum-to-zero columns. For a balanced
design this is the textbook table. For unequal cell sizes it is the type III sum of squares, as the runs showed
on a 2 x 3 design with cells of 3, 2, 4, 1, 3 and 2 numbers: with the interaction Origin gave 9.7878 for
factor A and 13.6434 for factor B, which are the type III values (type I gives 13.1499 for A and type II 9.7495);
without the interaction the numbers are 9.7495 and 13.9395, where type II and type III coincide. The same holds
on a second design with 3 x 2 levels. The Model row is total minus error with the
rank of the model minus 1 degrees of freedom, the Error row is the residual of the full model (so without the
interaction the interaction's sum of squares goes into the error), the Total row has N - 1 degrees of freedom.
R-squared is `SS model / SS total` and the root MSE the root of the error mean square.

**Edge cases.** With one number per cell and the interaction on, the error has no degrees of freedom and no F or
p is shown (Origin shows nothing there either); Sheaf also lists no means comparisons. Constant data give zero
sums of squares and no F. An empty cell (a combination of levels with no data) with the interaction on:
Origin's library does not stop but its table is inconsistent (it counts the empty cell in the degrees of freedom),
so Sheaf refuses and says which cell is empty; without the interaction an empty cell is fine, as long as the
levels are connected through the data (otherwise Sheaf refuses, the effects cannot be told apart).

**Means comparisons.** Tukey, Bonferroni, Sidak, Fisher LSD and Scheffe, as in section 2, for the levels of A, for
the levels of B and, with the interaction, for every one of the A x B cells (A changes slowest, so
A1B1, A1B2, ...). The mean of a level is the plain mean of its observations, the difference is `mean(j) - mean(i)`,
the standard error `sqrt(MSE (1/n_i + 1/n_j))` with MSE and its degrees of freedom from the Error row (the
marginal means are not adjusted for the other factor, which only matters in unbalanced designs). The statistic of
the t methods keeps the sign of the difference, the Tukey q and the Scheffe F do not. Origin's library numbers
the methods 1 Tukey, 2 Bonferroni, 3 Sidak, 4 Fisher, 5 Scheffe, which differs from the order in its header comment.
Holm methods (section 11) and Dunnett (section 15) are in the one-way ANOVA only. For constant data Origin's 0/0 turns into p = 0 and "significant" for
equal means; Sheaf says p = 1.

**Sheet.** `ANOVA2Way1`: one table of N, mean, SD and SEM for each level of A, each level of B, each cell (with the
interaction) and all the data; the ANOVA table; R-squared and root MSE; the comparison table. The descriptive
statistics of the cells that Origin's library returns are only right for data sorted by cell (our test data are
shuffled), so they were not used as a reference; Sheaf computes them from the cells directly.

## 8 Nonparametric tests

Origin: Statistics > Nonparametric Tests; X-Functions `mwtest`, `signrank1`, `signrank2`, `kwanova`. Their
reports have a table of descriptive statistics, a table of ranks and a table of test statistics; Sheaf puts the
numbers in one table row per test and in the result sheet. Missing values are ignored. Ranks: tied numbers get the
mean of the ranks they span (the "tie correction" of the variance below is then needed and was confirmed).

**Mann-Whitney** (`mwtest`; only the raw form, one column per sample, is taken; Origin also has indexed data).
`R1` is the rank sum of the first sample in the pooled ranks, `U = R1 - n1 (n1 + 1) / 2` (Origin prints the U of the
first sample, not the smaller one). Normal approximation: mean `n1 n2 / 2`, variance
`n1 n2 / 12 ((N + 1) - sum(t^3 - t) / (N (N - 1)))`, continuity correction 0.5. The printed Z is
`(U - mean + 0.5) / sd` for U below the mean and `- 0.5` above it. The asymptotic p-value of the lower tail
("F(x) < G(y)", the first sample tends to be smaller) is `Phi((U - mean + 0.5) / sd)`, of the upper tail
`1 - Phi((U - mean - 0.5) / sd)`, two-sided twice the smaller of the two, at most 1. With the "exact" option Origin
adds the exact p-value: the permutation distribution of the rank sum of the first sample, using the mean ranks
of the pooled data (so ties are handled exactly, not ignored): lower tail `P(R <= R1)`, upper `P(R >= R1)`,
two-sided twice the smaller, at most 1. The runs matched it to 1e-9, with ties and unequal sizes up to 60 and 65
numbers (the largest run; Origin showed no limit; the table of subset sums Sheaf builds is limited to 4e8 cells, beyond which Sheaf
reports the asymptotic p and says so). With the exact option Origin also prints a second number, the asymptotic
p-value, which for the upper tail uses the plain `1 - Phi(Z)` and not the corrected one of the run without the
option; the two-sided and lower values are the same in both runs. Sheaf copies this: with `exact` the upper
asymptotic p is `1 - Phi(Z)` of the printed Z (all upper-tail runs in `stats2_origin.rs` match to 1e-9). If all
numbers are equal Origin gives Z = 0 and p = 1 (all tails); a group of one number gives no result in Origin, Sheaf
computes the test.

**Wilcoxon signed rank** (`signrank1` against a hypothesized median, default 0, and `signrank2` for pairs). The
differences are `x - median` or `x - y` (rows with a missing number in either column are dropped), differences of
exactly zero are left out (the count of the rest is N), the absolute values are ranked with mean ranks and
`T+` is the sum of the ranks of the positive differences, which is the statistic Origin prints (for pairs, the
sum of the ranks of rows where the first column is larger). Mean `N (N + 1) / 4`, variance
`N (N + 1) (2N + 1) / 24 - sum(t^3 - t) / 48`. `Z = (T+ - mean + 0.5) / sd` for T+ below the mean and `- 0.5`
above it; the asymptotic p is `2 Phi(-|Z|)` (two-sided), `1 - Phi(Z)` (upper tail, "the first is larger") and
`Phi(Z)` (lower tail); the one-sided values use the same Z, unlike Mann-Whitney's. Origin always prints the exact
p-value next to the asymptotic one, when there are at most 80 non-zero differences (81 differences print the
asymptotic value alone, and five zero differences among 85 values bring it back, which is how the limit was found).
The exact p is computed over the sign patterns with the mean ranks, lower `P(T <= T+)`, upper `P(T >= T+)`,
two-sided twice the smaller, at most 1; the runs agree to 1e-9, including ties among the differences. If there is
nothing to rank, Origin prints T = 0, no Z and p = 1; Sheaf says so in a sentence. Origin's normal tail gives 0 beyond
about 8 standard deviations, ours is exact there (compared with an absolute 1e-12).

**Kruskal-Wallis** (`kwanova`; raw form, one column per group). `H = 12 / (N (N + 1)) sum(R_i^2 / n_i) - 3 (N + 1)`
divided by `1 - sum(t^3 - t) / (N^3 - N)`; chi-squared with k - 1 degrees of freedom. A group of one number is
allowed. If all numbers are equal H = 0 and p = 1. Origin has no exact option, and none is built.

Sheaf's tools: `Tool::MannWhitney` (tail, exact, alpha), `Tool::SignedRank` (one-sample with the median, or paired;
tail; whether to decide with the exact p; alpha) and `Tool::KruskalWallis`; sheets `MannWhitney1`, `SignedRank1`,
`KruskalWallis1`. Each gives a sentence with the statistic, p-value and decision.

## 9 Correlation coefficients

Origin: Statistics > Descriptive Statistics > Correlation Coefficient (`corrcoef`, at least two columns;
Pearson on by default, Spearman and Kendall optional; missing values pairwise (default) or listwise). The report
has, for each column, N, mean, SD, sum, minimum and maximum, and for each coefficient the matrix of r with the matrix
of p-values (significance) below it; separate worksheets (`pwks`, `swks`, `kwks`) hold the plain r matrices.

Confirmed on four columns with ties and several missing-value layouts (agreement to 1e-12 in r, 5e-5 in p):

| Coefficient | r | p-value (two-sided) |
|---|---|---|
| Pearson | the usual product-moment r | t test, `t = r sqrt((n - 2) / (1 - r^2))`, n - 2 degrees of freedom |
| Spearman | Pearson's r of the mean ranks (so ties are handled) | the same t test |
| Kendall | tau-b, `S / sqrt((n0 - n1) (n0 - n2))` with S = concordant minus discordant pairs | normal test of `S / sqrt(Var S)`, `Var S = (n (n - 1) (2n + 5) - sum t (t - 1) (2t + 5) - sum u (u - 1) (2u + 5)) / 18 + sum t (t - 1) sum u (u - 1) / (2 n (n - 1)) + sum t (t - 1) (t - 2) sum u (u - 1) (u - 2) / (9 n (n - 1) (n - 2))`, no continuity correction |

There is no p-value where r is exactly +1 or -1 (which includes every case of two pairs) and for the diagonal;
a constant column has no coefficients at all (its row and column are empty, including the diagonal). Pairwise
deletion uses, for each pair of columns, the rows where both have a number. With missing values Origin's
Kendall p-value differs from this formula by up to 17 % (re-examined 2026-11-13: for the four pairs with a missing row the variance Origin implies is 127.7, 165.0, 162.9 and 160.7 against 124.0, 163.0, 159.1 and 158.4 here; the exact permutation p, the full-column tie counts and tau-b scaling variants did not reproduce them, and with only four rounded values the rule was not found) in the pairs that lost rows (its tie bookkeeping is not
the textbook one and was not worked out); the coefficient itself agrees, and the Pearson and Spearman p-values
agree in all cases. Sheaf keeps the textbook formula and the test does not compare those Kendall p-values.

Sheaf's `Tool::Correlation` (coefficients chosen with flags, listwise or pairwise, alpha for the sentence that
names the significant pairs) writes `Correlation1`: the descriptive table, and for each coefficient the matrix of r
(variable names down the side, one column per variable), the matrix of p-values and, when pairs have different
counts, the matrix of N; the report table has one row per pair with N, r and p of every coefficient.
Kendall's score is counted in O(n log n) time (sort by one column, count the inversions of the other by a merge
sort, correct for ties), so hundreds of thousands of rows take well under a second.

Two-way ANOVA lists the pairwise comparison of the cells only when the design has at most 64 cells, because the
pairs grow with the square of the cells and Tukey's studentized range probabilities are costly. The factor
comparisons are always listed. Comparisons are left out (not a panic) when the data contain an infinite value.

## 10 Sign, two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov, median and Friedman tests; outlier tests; row statistics

Origin X-Functions `sign2`, `kstest2`, `mediantest`, `friedman`, `grubbs`, `qtest` and `rowstats`, run from
`tools/origin-ref/stats3/` (generators `g2.py` to `g5.py`, compact raw output `stats3-p*.compact.txt`, the
fixture `crates/calc/tests/fixtures/stats3_origin.rs` written by `stats3-make-fixture.py`, the test
`crates/calc/tests/origin_stats3_tests.rs`). About 200 runs on inputs we generated, with ties, missing values,
constant data and one or two numbers. Origin has no one-sample sign test X-Function (`sign2` is the paired
one); the other Nonparametric Tests entries of the index (`NPHKSamples`, `NPHPairSample`, `NPHTwoSample`) are
the dialog front ends and were not run. The report sheets have no header cells we could read through
automation, so the meaning of each number was inferred from the values (all inferences were then confirmed by
the 1e-9 agreement of the test).

**Paired sign test** (`sign2`; two columns, rows with a missing number in either are dropped; the `null`
argument is only a text). Pairs with equal numbers are left out: `N = S + T`, where `S` is the count of pairs
with the first number smaller (the statistic Origin prints) and `T` with the second smaller. At most 25 such
pairs: exact binomial p-value. With `m = min(S, T)` and `pm = P(Bin(N, 1/2) <= m)`: two-sided `min(1, 2 pm)`;
one-sided, "lower" is `pm` when `S < T` and `1 - pm` when `S > T`, "upper" the other way round, and both are 0.5
when `S = T` (so the one-sided values are not the textbook ones: the direction is chosen by which count is
larger). Origin's binomial tail is off by up to 1.4e-6 relative. From 26 pairs up: normal approximation with
continuity correction, `Z = (|S - N/2| - 1/2) / (sqrt(N) / 2)` (negative when `S = N/2`), two-sided
`min(1, 2 (1 - Phi(|Z|)))`, upper `1 - Phi(Z)`, lower `Phi(Z)` (here the direction does not matter: swapping the
columns gives the same one-sided values). Z is printed only then. All pairs equal: p = 1 for every tail. Fewer than
two pairs: no result. Tail names follow Origin's `F(x) < G(y)` / `F(x) > G(y)`; Sheaf's `Upper` is the latter.

**Two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov** (`kstest2`, raw form; it prints N1, N2, D, Z and p). `D+` is the largest
`F1 - F2`, `D-` the largest `F2 - F1` of the empirical distribution functions (evaluated at the distinct pooled
values), `D = max(D+, D-)`; the tail "upper" (`F(x) > G(y)`) uses `D+`, "lower" `D-`, two-sided `D`. `Z = sqrt(n1 n2 /
(n1 + n2)) D`. The two-sided p-value of a distance `D` is `P(D_{n1,n2} >= D)` of the continuous distribution
(lattice-path count, ties in the data are ignored even in the exact value: heavily tied samples matched to 1e-9)
when `n1 n2 <= 10000` (checked up to 100 x 100 exact and 60 x 200 not exact; the limit lies between 10000 and
12000), and above that `Q(Z + 0.5 / sqrt(max(n1, n2)))` with Kolmogorov's `Q(z) = 2 sum (-1)^(j-1) exp(-2 j^2 z^2)`
(fitted to six runs; the p-values agree to better than 1e-7 relative: 100 x 120, 300 x 400, 400 x 300, 250 x 50,
200 x 150, 120 x 100). A one-sided p-value is half of the two-sided p-value of that one-sided `D` (so 0.5 when
the distance is 0). Either sample with fewer than two numbers: no result.

**Median test** (`mediantest`, raw form). The overall median of all numbers is the ordinary median. Each group
is counted at or below the median and above it (a number equal to the median counts as below); `chi2 = sum
(O - E)^2 / E` over the `2 x k` table, no continuity correction and no Fisher test, `k - 1` degrees of freedom.
Cells with expected count 0 are skipped (all numbers equal: chi2 = 0, df = k - 1, p = 1). Groups of one number
are allowed; chi-square tail agrees to 2e-5 relative.

**Friedman ANOVA** (`friedman`, raw form: columns are treatments, rows blocks). Ranks within each row with mean
ranks for ties; `chi2 = 12 / (n k (k + 1)) sum R_j^2 - 3 n (k + 1)`, **no correction for ties** (the textbook
tie-corrected statistic differs from Origin's number; checked with ties in many rows), `k - 1` degrees of freedom,
chi-square p. All numbers equal: chi2 = 0, p = 1. The report also has N, minimum, quartiles, median, maximum, mean
rank and rank sum of each column. Origin writes **no report** when a row has a missing number or the columns
have different lengths, and Sheaf refuses too, with a sentence.

**Grubbs** (`grubbs`; inputs the data and a level 0.1, 0.05 or 0.01 offered, any level accepted). Missing values
are ignored but the index counts them as rows. `G = max |x - mean| / SD` of the point farthest from the mean (the
lower one when both ends are equally far); critical value `(n - 1) / sqrt(n) sqrt(t^2 / (n - 2 + t^2))` with `t`
the upper `alpha / (2 n)` quantile of Student's t with `n - 2` degrees of freedom; "significant" is `G >
critical`. The "approximate p-value" is `n * 2 * P(T > t_G)` with `t_G = G sqrt(n (n - 2) / ((n - 1)^2 - n G^2))`
and `n - 2` degrees of freedom; it is **not limited to 1** (5.06 was seen) and is not given when `G` has its
largest possible value `(n - 1) / sqrt(n)`. At least three numbers with a non-zero SD; two numbers or constant
data give no result. Origin's t quantile is off by up to 5e-6 relative, so critical values and p-values agree to
2e-5. The sentences are "At the 0.05 level, the highest (lowest) value X is a significant outlier" and "... there
is no significant outlier".

**Dixon's Q test** (`qtest`; levels 0.1, 0.05 and 0.01 only, 3 to 10 numbers; two numbers or more than ten give
no result, constant data: "All valid input data are equal"). `Q = gap / range` for the lowest value (gap to the
second lowest) and the highest value (gap to the second highest); the larger is tested, the lowest on a tie;
"significant" is `Q > critical`. Critical values (n = 3 to 10) at 0.1, 0.05, 0.01: 0.941, 0.970, 0.994; 0.765,
0.829, 0.926; 0.642, 0.710, 0.821; 0.560, 0.625, 0.740; 0.507, 0.568, 0.680; 0.468, 0.526, 0.634; 0.437, 0.493,
0.598; 0.412, 0.466, 0.568 (all 24 measured, the table is in `crates/calc/src/outlier.rs`).

**Statistics on Rows** (`rowstats`; outputs mean and SD by default, standard error, minimum, maximum, number of
points and sum optional). One number per worksheet row across the chosen columns; NaN ignored. A row with no
number gives missing values in every output including the count and the sum; one number has no SD and no SE.
Sheaf adds the chosen columns behind the last chosen column (`Tool::RowStatistics`, long names "Mean", "Standard
Deviation", "SE of Mean", "Minimum", "Maximum", "Number of Points", "Sum"). Origin's own dialog has more options
(for example row ranges as input) that were not measured.

Sheaf: `Tool::SignTest`, `Tool::KsTwoSample`, `Tool::MedianTest`, `Tool::Friedman`, `Tool::Grubbs`, `Tool::Dixon`
and `Tool::RowStatistics`; result sheets `SignTest1`, `KolmogorovSmirnov1`, `MedianTest1`, `Friedman1`,
`Grubbs1`, `Dixon1`; command line `analyze sign | ks2 | mediantest | friedman | grubbs | dixon | rowstats`; menu
entries Statistics > Nonparametric Tests > Two Sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov Test, Mood's Median Test, Friedman ANOVA,
Paired Sample Sign Test and Statistics > Descriptive Statistics > Statistics on Rows, Grubbs Test, Dixon's Q-test
(labels from the X-Function descriptions). Not done: the indexed input forms, the outlier box plot (`box`),
Origin's report layout, outlier tests that remove several points in turn.

## 11 Holm comparisons of the one-way ANOVA

Origin's statistics library (the function the one-way ANOVA tool calls for its means comparisons) numbers the
methods 1 Tukey, 2 Bonferroni, 3 Sidak, 4 Fisher, 5 Scheffe, 6 Dunnett, 7 Bonferroni-Holm, 8 Sidak-Holm.
Run from `tools/origin-ref/stats4/` (helper `stats4-ref.c`, generator `g1.py`, raw output `holm1.out.txt`, fixture
`crates/calc/tests/fixtures/stats4_origin.rs` by `make-fixture1.py`, test `crates/calc/tests/origin_stats4_tests.rs`):
28 inputs of ours (2 to 8 groups, unequal sizes, a group of one number, tied means, missing values, three
significance levels, and four found by a search for the case below), each with both Holm methods: 54 comparison
tables, 386 pairs.

- Every pair gets the plain pairwise t statistic `(mean_j - mean_i) / sqrt(MSE (1/n_i + 1/n_j))` (**with its sign**,
  unlike the other methods in the one-way table, which show the size) and its unadjusted two-sided p-value on
  the error degrees of freedom.
- The table is **sorted by that p-value**, smallest first (the pair index column shows where each came from; pairs
  are numbered (1,2), (1,3), (2,3), (1,4), ... from 0). Exactly equal p-values (symmetric data) come in an order we
  could not predict from the data; Sheaf keeps the pair order.
- The pair of rank `r` (from 0, `m` pairs in all) is judged at `alpha / (m - r)` (Bonferroni-Holm) or
  `1 - (1 - alpha)^(1 / (m - r))` (Sidak-Holm); these levels are returned in an extra column. The verdict is the
  step-down rule: significant only if this pair and every pair before it in the list have `p < level`. (The four
  searched inputs have a second p-value below its level after a first that is not; Origin says "not significant"
  for both.)
- No confidence limits (Sheaf shows the level column in their place); the degrees-of-freedom column is empty (0).
- Constant data (error mean square 0): the statistic and p-value are blank and every pair is "significant"; Sheaf
  says p = 1 and not significant, as for the other methods.
- Agreement: mean differences and standard errors to 1e-12 relative; the statistic to 1e-12; p-values to 9.1e-6
  relative (Origin's t tail); levels to 1e-9; all 386 verdicts equal.
- The two-way ANOVA library call with the Holm numbers returns the sorted unadjusted statistics and p-values but
  no levels and no verdicts (the field is always 0), so Sheaf offers Holm in the one-way ANOVA only (menu, `sheaf
  analyze anova --comparison holm-bonferroni | holm-sidak`) and refuses it in the two-way ANOVA.
- **Dunnett** (method 6): the same call returns the differences and standard errors but no statistic, p-value or
  limits for any input, in the one-way and the two-way library functions, and the library has no way to name the
  control group. The installed Origin gave nothing to copy; Sheaf's Dunnett is the textbook test checked against scipy (section 15).

- **Layout of the result sheet**: column 1 holds one row per X bin, long name `Bin Ends of A` (the X column's short

  fell into a bin (5 of 15 binned points gives 33.333), not of all points.

- **Outlier bins** (`border.outleft`, `outright`): a bin `< min` labelled `min` (bin ends) and a bin `>= max`

- **Automatic range, automatic size**: the size is the 1D one (section 3, `auto_bin_size` with the given limits where

### 15.1 Dunnett in One-Way ANOVA

Dunnett is an option of "Means comparison" next to Tukey, Bonferroni, Sidak, Fisher LSD, Scheffe and the two Holm
methods of section 11 (menu: Statistics > ANOVA > One-Way ANOVA; `sheaf analyze anova --comparison dunnett
[--control N]`). It is refused in the two-way ANOVA because it needs one control group. Origin's library has a
method number for Dunnett (6) but the call gave only differences and standard errors, no statistic, p-value or interval (section 11), so what follows is the textbook
definition checked against scipy.

- **Dunnett** (`MeansComparison::Dunnett`, `calc::dunnett`, `compare_to_control`): each group other than the control is compared with the
  control group (the first chosen column, or the one named by `--control` / "Control group"). Rows are listed in the
  order of the groups, labelled "group - control"; `diff = mean(group) - mean(control)`, `SE = sqrt(MSE (1/n_g +
  1/n_c))`, `t = diff / SE` (shown as `|t|`). The p-value is two-sided and adjusted for the whole family:
  `P(max_j |T_j| >= |t|)` for the multivariate t with `df` error degrees of freedom whose correlations are
  `corr(T_i, T_j) = l_i l_j`, `l_j = sqrt(n_j / (n_j + n_c))` (this holds for unequal group sizes too). The simultaneous
  confidence limits are `diff -/+ d SE` where `d` solves `P(max_j |T_j| <= d) = 1 - alpha` (one `d` for all rows).
  The family is the groups other than the control, so with one treatment Dunnett equals the two-sided t-test.
- **The multivariate t integral** is evaluated by a double Gauss-Legendre integral, not by Monte Carlo. The
  one-factor structure turns the `m`-dimensional integral into
  `Int f_S(s) Int phi(x) Prod_j [Phi((c s - l_j x)/r_j) - Phi((-c s - l_j x)/r_j)] dx ds` with `r_j = sqrt(1 - l_j^2)`
  and `f_S` the density of `sqrt(chi-square(df)/df)` (Dunnett 1955; Genz and Bretz 2009 for the same reduction). Above
  5000 degrees of freedom `s = 1` (the density of `s` is then a spike at 1; the test
  `large_degrees_of_freedom_use_the_normal_limit` bounds the jump across 5000 degrees of freedom by 1e-4, and no
  scipy fixture reaches that range: the largest has 156). The error of the panels used is below 1e-12.
- **Tolerances** (`scipy_stats_compare.rs`): scipy integrates the same distribution by quasi Monte Carlo, with an
  error of about 1e-4 in the p-value (fixed by a seed, but not reproducible to more digits, and poor in relative
  terms below 1e-5); our p-values are compared with scipy's to 5e-4 absolute and their confidence limits to 0.02. To pin
  the integration to 1e-9 the generator also evaluates the same double integral with its own, much finer
  vectorised rule (different nodes, 64-point panels) and the tests compare p-values and the critical value `d` with
  that to 1e-9 absolute. That second reference checks our implementation of the formula; the formula itself is
  validated by the agreement with scipy's independent Monte Carlo. Dunnett statistics agree with scipy to 1e-9.

### 15.2 Cross tabulation

Statistics > Descriptive Statistics > Cross Tabulation; `sheaf analyze crosstab --y ROW_COLUMN,COLUMN_COLUMN`. Two
columns of any type (text or numbers); each distinct value is a level. Rows where one of the two cells is empty are
left out (and counted in a note). Levels are listed sorted: numbers from small to large when all are numbers,
otherwise text by character order ignoring case (A before a).

The result sheet has three blocks next to each other: the table of counts with a "Total" row and column; one row per
cell with Count, Expected (`row total * column total / N`), Row %, Column % and Total % (each can be switched off);
and Statistic/Value pairs of the tests:

| Test | Statistic | df | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pearson chi-square | `sum (O - E)^2 / E` | `(r - 1)(c - 1)` | upper tail of chi-square |
| Likelihood ratio | `2 sum O ln(O / E)` (empty cells add 0) | same | same |
| Yates (2 x 2 only) | Pearson with each count moved 0.5 (at most to E) towards E | 1 | same |
| Fisher exact (2 x 2 only) | odds ratio `a d / (b c)` (infinite where `b c = 0`) | | two-sided, left (`P(X <= a)`), right (`P(X >= a)`) |

Fisher's two-sided p-value adds the probabilities of all tables with the same margins that are no more likely than
the observed one (relative tolerance 1e-9 on ties). A note warns when cells have an expected count below 5. A table
with fewer than two levels in a variable is refused, and so is a variable with more than 200 distinct levels (a column
of numbers or identifiers is not a category). Rows hidden by the sheet filter and masked cells are left out, as in the
other tools; the cross tabulation has no X column, so the "X from ... to" row range is refused (the window does not
offer it). Agreement with scipy `chi2_contingency` (`correction=False`,
`lambda_="log-likelihood"`) and `fisher_exact`: 1e-9 relative on 9 tables, including zero cells.

### 16.1 Distribution fit

Five families, each fitted to each chosen column by maximum likelihood (missing values are ignored; at least two
numbers with some spread). Lognormal, exponential, gamma and Weibull have their threshold fixed at 0, as
`scipy.stats.<dist>.fit(x, floc=0)` does, so they need positive numbers (the exponential accepts 0). A family that
cannot take a column is left out for that column with a note; if none can, the run is refused.

Data without spread are refused, not fitted: numbers that are all equal, or so close together that the estimate
would not be a finite number (for the gamma, a shape above 1e10, a coefficient of variation below about 1e-5, is
taken as no spread), give "no spread"; an estimate that is not finite or not above zero where it must be (numbers
of the order of 1e300 or 1e-300) gives "cannot be fitted". The fit never panics and always terminates, and
`cdf` of the fitted distribution returns NaN for parameters outside its domain.

| Family | Parameters | Estimate |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | mean, SD | sample mean; SD with divisor n (computed scaled by the largest deviation, so that squares neither underflow nor overflow) |
| Lognormal | mu, sigma of the logarithm | mean and SD (divisor n) of ln x |
| Exponential | scale (the mean) | sample mean |
| Gamma | shape k, scale | k solves `ln k - digamma(k) = ln(mean) - mean(ln x)` (Newton), scale = mean / k |
| Weibull | shape c, scale | c solves the likelihood equation (bisection on ln c), scale from c |

Reported per row: N, the parameters, log-likelihood `ln L`, `AIC = 2k - 2 ln L`, `BIC = k ln n - 2 ln L` (k = number
of fitted parameters, the fixed threshold not counted), Kolmogorov-Smirnov D and p, Anderson-Darling A2 (and an
approximate p for normal and lognormal). The notes name the family with the lowest AIC. KS p is for a distribution
known in advance (as `scipy.stats.kstest`), so it is too large when the parameters were estimated from the same
data; this is said in the note. A2 is `-n - (1/n) sum (2i-1) (ln F(x_i) + ln(1 - F(x_(n+1-i))))` against the fitted
distribution; for an exponential fit to data that contain 0 it is infinite (F(0) = 0 under the fit), not a finite number
from a floor. The A2 p-value uses the D'Agostino-Stephens formula for estimated mean and SD, applied to the
normal and to the lognormal (on the logarithm). That formula is for A2 computed with the sample SD (divisor n - 1),
as the Normality Test does, while the reported A2 uses the maximum likelihood SD (divisor n); the p-value is
therefore taken from A2 recomputed with the n - 1 SD, so it equals the p-value the Normality Test gives for the
same column (tested). No p is given for the other families. The KS p-value is exact (the matrix method) up to
n = 1000 and, above that, the asymptotic series with Stephens' correction (measured against scipy's `kstwo.sf`: off by up to 0.0042
at n = 1001, 0.0030 at n = 2000 and 0.0014 at n = 10000, worst near `sqrt(n) d` = 0.67); for a p-value below 1e-7
the series is used from n = 140 on (accurate to 1e-9 there).

Result sheet `DistFit1`: columns Data, Distribution, N, Parameter 1 (name), Value 1, Parameter 2 (name),
Value 2 (empty for the exponential), Log-likelihood, AIC, BIC, KS D, KS Prob, Anderson-Darling A2 and Prob.

### 16.2 Probability plot and Q-Q plot

Both use the plotting positions of `scipy.stats.probplot` (Filliben's medians of the uniform order statistics:
`(i - 0.3175) / (n + 0.365)`, with `1 - 0.5^(1/n)` and `0.5^(1/n)` at the ends).

- **Q-Q plot** (`QQPlot1`): X is the theoretical quantile at the plotting position, Y the ordered data; the
  reference line is the least-squares line through the points (slope estimates the scale, intercept the location,
  `r` printed). Families with a shape (lognormal sigma, gamma, Weibull) take the shape fitted by maximum
  likelihood; the quantiles are those of the standard form, as `probplot(..., sparams=shape)`. The gamma quantile is solved by bisection on ln x when the library routine cannot reach the lower tail (shape below about 1 with a small p; tested for shapes 0.05 to 2). A linear graph.
- **Probability plot** (`ProbPlot1`): X is the ordered data, Y the plotting position in percent; the Y axis is a
  probability axis (normal and lognormal use the normal probability scale, Weibull and exponential the Weibull
  scale; gamma the normal one), the X axis is logarithmic for lognormal, Weibull and (positive data) exponential.
  The reference line is the distribution function of the maximum likelihood fit over the range of the data, kept
  to 0.01 % to 99.99 %.

Both make a new graph (a scatter plot and a line plot per column, the line in the colour of its points) unless
"Make a graph" is off, plus the result sheet with the plotted columns and a table (N, parameters or slope,
intercept, r). One undo step removes sheet and graph.

Auto Apply is not offered for these two windows (the checkbox is disabled): each run makes a new sheet and a new
graph, so running at every option change would pile them up; they run on OK and Apply.

### 16.3 Checked against scipy, tolerances

optimizer stops 1e-5 to 2.5e-5 short of the maximum, so against its fit the parameters agree to 1e-4; against the

### 17.1 What Origin does (measured)

- **Matrix.** Correlation matrix (default) or covariance matrix with divisor n - 1. Eigenvalues in descending
  order, percent of variance `100 lambda / sum(lambda)` (Origin prints two decimals), cumulative percent.
- **Missing values.** Listwise: a row with a missing number in any column is left out entirely; N, mean and SD of
  every column are over the complete rows (the "missing" count is still per column). Pairwise: N, mean and SD are per
  column over all its numbers, and each matrix entry uses the rows where both columns have a number, with the means
  of those rows. The scores of a row that is not used are empty (listwise); with pairwise exclusion rows with a gap
  also have empty scores (the run shows scores only for complete rows).
- **Loadings are the eigenvectors** (unit length), not eigenvectors times sqrt of the eigenvalue.
- **Sign.** Origin makes the components of each eigenvector add up to a positive number (checked on 8 data sets,
  4 or 5 variables, both matrices); Sheaf does the same. A component whose sum is zero up to rounding would be
  ambiguous; none occurred.
- **Scores** are `(x - mean)` (covariance) or `(x - mean) / SD` (correlation) times the eigenvectors; `std` divides
  each score column by `sqrt(eigenvalue)`, giving unit variance.
- **Equality of eigenvalues, covariance analysis only.** For each k, the test that the last `p - k + 1` eigenvalues
  are equal: Bartlett's chi-square `(n - 1 - (2p + 5)/6) (q ln(mean) - sum ln lambda)` over the `q` eigenvalues
  left, `(q + 2)(q - 1)/2` degrees of freedom; the last row is 0, 0, 0. `n` is the largest column count (also with
  pairwise exclusion).
- **Report sheet** (a tree of titled tables; `rt`), measured from the pictures and the cell dump: title "Principal
  Component Analysis (date)", then **Notes** (X-Function, User Name, Time, Data Filter), **Input Data** ("Variables":
  Data and Range, one row per column named by its short name), **Descriptive Statistics** (N analysis, N missing,
  Mean, Standard Deviation), **Correlation Matrix** (always the correlation matrix, also for a covariance analysis),
  **Eigenvalues of the Correlation Matrix** or **of the Covariance Matrix** (Eigenvalue, Percentage of Variance and
  Cumulative as text with two decimals and a percent sign; for covariance also Chisq, Degrees of Freedom,
  Significance Level, and a blue remark "At the 0.05 level, eigenvalues from 1 to 4 are significantly equal."),
  **Extracted Eigenvectors** (Coefficients of PC1 ... PCk, rows named by the short names), then the embedded graphs
  **Scree Plot**, **Loading Plot**, **Biplot** (and **Scores Plot** when asked). The scores are in a sheet of their
  own (`rd`: columns "Principal Component 1 ..", comment "Scores") and the data of the graphs in a third (`rdplot`).
- **The dialog's plot switches** (the X-Function's options): `screeplot` (default on), `lplot` Loading Plot (on),
  `splot` Score Plot (off), `biplot` (on), `xcomp` and `ycomp` the components on the axes (1 and 2), `npc` number of
  components, `label` observation labels (not run). With all four on, the windows are named "Scree Plot", "Loading
  Plot", "Scores Plot" and "Biplot", in that creation order.
- **Scree plot.** Eigenvalues against the component number: blue squares joined by a red line, bottom and left axes
  only, horizontal axis "Principal Component Number" from 0.5 to p + 0.5 with step 1, vertical axis "Eigenvalues" from
  0; no legend.
- **Loading plot.** One vector from the origin to the eigenvector coefficients of the two chosen components for each
  variable, thin blue lines with the variable's short name in blue at the tip, no symbols, black lines through zero,
  a closed frame without ticks on top and right, both axes fixed from -1 to 1 with step 0.2 (also for a covariance
  analysis, whose eigenvectors are also of unit length), titled "Principal Component 1" and "Principal Component 2"
  (the long names of the score columns); no legend. Its data (`rdplot`): the component number and eigenvalue, then
  for the vectors two columns of zeros, the two coefficient columns and the labels.
- **Scores plot.** The scores of the two components as small red squares, black lines through zero, closed frame,
  automatic axes, the same titles; no legend.
- **Biplot.** Two layers: the scores as in the scores plot against the bottom and left axes (the automatic limits
  of the scores: for example -2 to 4 and -2 to 2), the loading vectors against the **top and right axes, drawn in
  blue**, whose limits are those of the bottom and left axes **times `max |loading| / min(|smallest score|, |largest
  score|)`** of the component on that axis, so that zero lies on the same line in both and the longest loading reaches
  the nearer end of the scores. Fits 13 of the 14 axes of the seven runs to the six digits Origin shows (the one miss is in 17.4; limits such as
  -0.71618 to 1.43236 and -1.202706 to 1.202706 from scores -1.628..2.967 and -1.421..1.623 and loadings up to 0.583
  and 0.854); the scores are the standardized ones when `std` is on, and those of the rows without missing values
  with listwise exclusion.

- **Area**: the trapezoid area (the Mann-Whitney statistic, ties one half), as Sheaf already had.
- **Standard error**: neither DeLong's nor Hanley and McNeil's exponential approximation. It is
  `sqrt((A (1 - A) + (n_neg - 1) (Q1 - A^2) + (n_pos - 1) (Q2 - A^2)) / (n_pos n_neg))` with
  `Q1 = integral of sensitivity^2 d(1 - specificity)` and `Q2 = integral of specificity^2 d(sensitivity)` along
  the straight segments of the curve, so a segment of tied values counts `dx (y0^2 + y0 y1 + y1^2) / 3`. It
  reproduces all four to the last digit (0.077493787947229317 with ties, 0.10327314252246217,
  0.18708286933869706 and 0 without). Sheaf now uses it.
- **Interval**: `A -/+ 1.96 SE`, **not limited to [0, 1]** (UCL 1.0503 and 1.0419, LCL -0.0667). Copied.
- **Asymptotic Prob**: two-sided normal p-value of `(A - 0.5) / SE0` with `SE0 = sqrt((n_pos + n_neg + 1) /
  (12 n_pos n_neg))`, the standard error when the area is 0.5 (0.0074055 where the observed SE would give
  2e-7). Copied; Sheaf's `Z` is that statistic.
- **Curve** ("Interpolation of data points"): one row per distinct value plus one, from (1, 1) to (0, 0),
  columns cut-off, 1 - specificity, sensitivity. The cut-offs are the midpoints between neighbouring
  distinct values (0.15 between 0.1 and 0.2) and, at the ends, the smallest value - 1 and the largest + 1
  (-0.3 and 5.8 for data from 0.7 to 4.8), or 0 and 1 when every value lies in [0, 1]. Copied (Sheaf keeps
  its order from (0, 0) to (1, 1)); with "Positive v.s. Low" the same rule mirrored (not measured).
- Not measured: Speed Mode, "Positive v.s. Low", text states, several data columns, other confidence levels.

## 20 Kaplan-Meier estimator (measured against Origin 2016)

Statistics > Survival Analysis > Kaplan-Meier Estimator. Origin's X-Function is `kaplanmeier` (`irng` time, censor
and optional group columns, `censor` the censoring value, `quartile`, `mean`, `logrank`, `breslow`, `tarone`;
outputs `rt` report sheet and `rd` survival-function sheet). Runs: `tools/origin-ref/kaplan-suite1.lines.txt` (14
cases with ties and 4 censored, one sample and two interleaved groups, with the three rank tests) and
`kaplan-suite2.lines.txt` (12 cases whose last two are censored or events, and 19 cases in three groups, one of
them a single case); `kaplan-probe.lines.txt` was a first look. Raw outputs are local
(`re-workspace/scratch/t29/km1.out`, `km3.out`). The numerics are `crates/calc/src/survival.rs` (the rules are in its
header), the tool `crates/core/src/analysis/survival.rs`, the command line `sheaf analyze kaplan-meier`, the window
Statistics > Survival Analysis > Kaplan-Meier Estimator... (`dialogs/multivariate_options.rs`).

### 20.1 What Origin does (measured)

- **Table.** One row per case, sorted by time; at tied times the events come before the censored cases (a case censored
  at `t` is at risk at `t`). The survival estimate and its standard error are printed on the last of the tied event rows
  only (other rows show `--`), a `+` marks censored cases, and the running number of events and the number of cases
  left at risk after the row follow.
- **Estimate.** `S(t) = prod (1 - d/n)`; standard error Greenwood's `S sqrt(sum d / (n (n - d)))`, 0 once `S` is 0.
  The `rd` sheet holds the step function (time and survival, the start `(0, 1)` first).
- **Quartiles** (25, 50, 75 %): the estimate is the smallest event time with `S <= 1 - p`. Its lower and upper limits are
  the smallest event times at which the **linear** limits `S -/+ z se` are at or below `1 - p` (lower limit of the quartile
  from the lower limit of `S`, upper limit from the upper one; z for 95 %). When the upper limit is never reached Origin
  gives the largest event time (seen once, for a quartile whose estimate is reached; for a quartile never reached Sheaf gives no limits, unmeasured).
- **Mean** is the area under the curve up to the largest time, **censored or not**. Standard error `sqrt(f sum A^2 d /
  (n (n - d)))`, `A` the area to the right of each event time, with `f = D / (D - 1)` (`D` the number of events) **only when
  the curve reaches 0** (the largest time is an event); with a censored largest time `f = 1` (six runs). Limits mean -/+ z se.
- **Tests of groups** (two or more groups): log-rank (weight 1), Breslow (generalised Wilcoxon, weight `n`) and
  Tarone-Ware (weight `sqrt(n)`); chi-square with `groups - 1` degrees of freedom, hypergeometric variance. Measured with
  two and three groups; the probability agrees with the exact chi-square tail to 4e-8 (Origin's own tail routine).
- **Confidence limits of the curve** (`sfci:=1`; measured with 90, 95 and 99 percent, one and two groups, runs
  `kaplan-plots2.lines.txt`, `kaplan-plots3.lines.txt`): the survival-function sheet (`rd`) has per group the columns
  "Time", "Survival(g)", "95% UCI" and "95% LCI" (the level is the `conf` option; comment "Survival Function"), on the
  event rows, the start `(0, 1, 1, 1)` first. The limits are the **linear limits `S -/+ z se`, not cut off** at 1 or 0
  (the first upper limit of the two-group example is 1.1163677216739256, a lower limit -0.15141395101436683), 0 and 0
  once the curve is 0. The call with `sfci:=1` returned false (nothing drawn) with `plotinone:=1`; with `plotinone:=0`
  it made one graph per group. (The other plot options `omsf`, `hazard`, `lsf` write further column groups: 1 - S,
  the hazard and ln S; not read further.)
- **Survival graph** (picture): window "Survival Function" (a second group "Survival Function(2)"), boxed title
  "Survival Function" with a shadow, a black step line "Survival(1)" (red for the second group when two groups share
  a graph), the limits dotted and thin ("95% UCI", "95% LCI"), horizontal axis the time column from -5 left of 0 on, the
  vertical axis "Survival Function" fixed from -0.02 to 1.02 (so a limit beyond 0 or 1 is cut by the frame), no marks for
  censored cases, a legend at the top right.
- **Report layout** (pictures of the report sheet): title "Kaplan-Meier Estimates (date)", **Notes** (X-Function, User
  Name, Time, Data Filter), **Input Data** (Data and Range of the Time Range, Censor Range and Grouping Range
  (Optional)), **Summary of Event and Censored Values** (rows 1, 2, ... per group: Total, Events, Censored, Percent
  Censored as a fraction; note "Censored Value(s): 1"), then per group **Survival Analysis of Time (g)** with
  **Kaplan-Meier Survival Estimates** (Time, Status, Cumulative Survival, Standard Error, Cumulative Event, Number
  Remaining; a "+" and "--" on censored rows; note "Observations with status as + are censored ones."), **Quartile
  Estimates** (Percent Failures 25, 50, 75; Estimate; "95% LCL"; "95% UCL"), **Mean Estimate** (Estimate, Std.Error, "95%
  LCL", "95% UCL"; note "Estimation is underestimated because the largest observation is censored." when it is),
  then **Survival Plots** (the graph) and **Test of Equality over Groups** (rows Log Rank, Breslow, Tarone-Ware;
  Chi-Square, df, Prob>Chi-Square).

### 20.2 What Sheaf does

The chosen columns are the time, the censor column and, optionally, a grouping column (numbers or text), in that order.
A case is censored where its censor cell equals the censoring value (default 1; numbers compare by value, text as text);

  0.49720915042233 to 0.69670523129712; 1 of 5: 0.00505076337946806 to 0.716417936118089). 20 of 20 two-sided:
  lower 0.831566529016915, upper `--` (all successes has no upper limit). 0 successes: the dialog does not accept

- Power analysis (Actual Power, Hypothetical Power; runs o1 to o4): a section "Powers" with the table Alpha, Sample

  2.0412414523193, two-sided p 0.041226833337006), the pooled one `sqrt(p q (1/n1 + 1/n2))` with "Use pooled p for
  test" (z 2, p 0.045500263896185). With a nonzero test difference the pooled box has no effect (0.1: z
  1.0206207261597 = `(0.2 - 0.1) / sqrt(.6 .4 / 50 + .4 .6 / 50)`, p 0.30743416593004, with or without the box). The
  sign of z is kept and nothing is clipped, unlike the one-sample test (20 of 50 against 30 of 50: z
  -2.0412414523193, two-sided p 0.041226833337006, upper p 0.9793865833315, pooled lower p 0.022750131948092 for
  z -2). Tails: upper `1 - Phi(z)` (pooled 30 of 50 against 20 of 50: 0.022750131948092), lower `Phi(z)`
  (0.9793865833315 unpooled). Equal proportions give z 0 and p 1 (20 of 25 against 20 of 25). Both samples all
  successes (10 of 10 against 8 of 8) give Z and P `--` (no spread), difference 0, limits 0 and 0, Fisher's p 1.
- Intervals: always the unpooled Wald interval of the difference, not cut at 0 (cut at -1 and 1), whichever z was
  used: 95 % 0.0079635329457877 to 0.39203646705421 for 30 of 50 against 20 of 50; the headline `95% LCL`
  and `UCL` equal the table row of the level `1 - alpha`. Upper-tailed test: lower limits only, upper limit printed
  `1` (90 95 99: 0.074434103414031, 0.0388379164961065, -0.0279346102243397); lower-tailed: lower limit `-1`, upper
  limits 0.325565896585969, 0.361162083503893, 0.42793461022434; negative difference 20 of 50 against 30 of 50
  (95 %): -0.39203646705421 to -0.0079635329457877. Fisher's section shows the same numbers as the normal section.
- Fisher's exact p (hypergeometric; two-sided: every table no more likely than the observed one): 30 of 50 against
  20 of 50 0.071342403485151 (two-sided), 0.035671201744004 (upper), 0.98634419171458 (lower); 3 of 10 against 1 of
  8 0.5882352938664 (exact 10/17); 10 of 10 against 5 of 10 0.032507739937749; 15 of 40 against 25 of 35
  0.0051489221075077. Origin's values differ from the exact ones by up to 3e-10 (the same table in the mirrored
  order gave 0.98634419171744): tolerance 5e-10 absolute.
- Warning line under the normal approximation's hypotheses (Origin's text says that with fewer than 10 successes
  and a sample size below successes + 10 the approximation might be unsatisfactory; ours is worded differently): it
  appears when some sample has fewer than 10 failures (Sheaf also warns for fewer than 10 successes, unmeasured): 3/10 and 1/8, 10/10 and 5/10,
  10/10 and 8/8, 20/25 and 20/25 (5 failures), 3/10 and 30/50, 30/50 and 3/10 all show it; 30/50 and 20/50, 12/30
  and 30/50, and 15/40 and 25/35 (the second sample has exactly 10 failures) do not. Every sample that showed it has fewer than 10 failures, and
  every one without it has 10 or more. No run had fewer than 10 successes with 10 or more failures (nor exactly 10
  successes), so Sheaf's "fewer than 10 successes" half of the rule is an unmeasured guess; Origin's own text only
  describes the failures side. The one-sample report's warning follows the same rule.
- Power analysis (Actual Power, Hypothetical Power; significance level of its own, default 0.05): table Alpha, Sample
  Size, Power. "Actual Power" gives one row per distinct sample size, each taken as the size of both samples (30
  of 50 against 20 of 50: one row, 50; 15 of 40 against 25 of 35: rows 40 and 35), then the hypothetical sizes (50 100
  200) as typed. The test difference and the pooled box do not change it (0.1 gave the same 0.516323421027342). With
  `d = p1 - p2`, `pbar = (p1 + p2) / 2`, `se0 = sqrt(2 pbar (1 - pbar) / n)`, `se1 = sqrt((p1 q1 + p2 q2) / n)`:
  two-sided `Phi((d - z se0) / se1) + Phi((-d - z se0) / se1)` with the `1 - alpha/2` quantile (matched to 1e-12:
  0.516323421027342 for 50 and 0.278367227355963 for alpha 0.01; 15/40 against 25/35: 0.87616102837545 (40),
  0.828106373863776 (35), 0.938038580207161 (50), 0.998814261304273 (100), 0.999999877623331 (200)); one-sided:
  upper tail only `Phi((-d - z se0) / se1)` (30/50 against 20/50: 9.96062005463871E-05, with test alpha 0.05 or 0.1),
  lower tail only `Phi((d - z se0) / se1)` (15/40 against 25/35: 3.01679803625835E-07 at n 40, 8.72825092768114E-07 at
  n 35), both with the `1 - alpha` quantile, as for the one-sample test.
- Not entered: 0 successes (see 21.1). Unmeasured: the Raw and Indexed input forms, whether the headline LCL and UCL
  columns exist with the intervals unticked (every run had them ticked), the pooled box together with the power
  analysis for unequal sample sizes (the power formula always used the average proportion `pbar`).

### 21.5 Sheaf

`analyze` for the proportion tests reads no column (the first chosen column only names the workbook). The result
sheet is named `VarTest1` or `PropTest1`. The proportion sheet is laid out as columns: the normal section (Sample,
Success, N, Sample P, Difference, Estimate for difference, Z, P, LCL, UCL, level table), Fisher's section when
ticked (the same summary, Exact P, LCL, UCL, level table), and the power table (Alpha, Sample Size, Power); the
hypotheses and warnings go to the notes. The CLI takes `--power`, `--power-sizes 50,100,200` and `--power-alpha`
beside `--exact`, `--pooled` and `--levels`. The dialogs have the Power Analysis group. Tolerances of the fixtures:
1e-6 relative except the F quantiles (1e-5) and the exact p (5e-10 absolute; tiny exact p of the one-sample test
likewise).

## 22 Graphical Summary (T40)

Statistics > Descriptive Statistics > Graphical Summary. Numerics in `crates/calc/src/graph_summary.rs`, tool in
`crates/core/src/analysis/summary.rs`, window in `crates/app/src/dialogs/analysis.rs`
(`AnalysisKind::GraphSummary`), CLI `sheaf analyze graphsummary` (MCP: the bridged tool `graphsummary`).
Documentation facts only, nothing measured: Origin 2026 is not available to the sessions that built this.

### 22.1 What Origin does (documentation, not measured)

- Inputs: one or more columns (or a data table), optional Group column(s), Confidence Interval (%) level, Graph
  Arrangement: Separate Graphs, Per Variable, Per Group, All in One. [W:origin-help/graphical-summary]
- Separate Graphs: for each variable-group pair a graph with three plots (histogram with a distribution curve,
  confidence interval plot, probability plot) and three tables "Description Statistics", "##% Confidence Intervals"
  and "Normality Test", titled by variable and group. [W:origin-help/graphical-summary; blog.originlab.com/graphical-summary]
- Per Variable and Per Group draw a histogram with curve, a box chart with outliers and a CI plot with a panel per
  group or variable and one summary table; All in One puts all subsets in one graph. [same]
- The table shows mean and median with their confidence intervals; the summary includes the Anderson-Darling normality
  test (2026 release). [W:blog; press release]
- Not stated by Origin: the full statistic list, the default level, the destination of the outputs, the curve, the
  p-value method of the test, the interval formulas.

### 22.2 What Sheaf does (Sheaf's choices)

- **Arrangement**: only Separate Graphs. The other three are in the enum (`GraphArrangement`) and the dialog (greyed,
  with a hover note) and are refused by the tool and by `--arrangement` with "not available yet".
- **Groups**: any number of group columns (numbers or text); a group is one combination of their values. Rows with an
  empty group cell are left out. The group key is exact, not the 4-digit text of other tools: a numeric cell is kept
  as its f64 value (so 2023.5 and 2024 are two groups, -0 equals 0) and labelled with its full round-trip number
  (`2023.5`); a text cell is its trimmed text. Keys are ordered per column, in the order of the group columns, by a
  total order: numeric cells first, by `f64::total_cmp`, then text cells by bytes; numeric-ness comes from the cell
  type, never from parsing text, so "nan", "inf" or "1a" are plain text. Variable-group pairs with fewer than two numbers are left out with a note; at least one must remain.
  A variable-group whose mean, SD or variance is not finite (values such as 1e300 and -1e300) keeps its table rows
  but gets no graph, with a note, like the constant sample.
- **Statistics** (the Descriptive Statistics tool's own `calc::stats`, not redefined): N, missing, mean, standard
  deviation (n - 1), SE of the mean, variance, bias-corrected skewness and kurtosis, minimum, Q1, median, Q3, maximum
  (Origin's quartile rule of `calc::quantile_origin`).
- **Confidence level**: percent, default 95, between 0 and 100 (exclusive).
- **CI of the mean**: Student t, `mean +/- t(1 - a/2, n-1) * SE` (`calc::mean_confidence_limits`).
- **CI of the standard deviation**: chi-square, `s sqrt((n-1)/chi2(1 - a/2, n-1))` to `s sqrt((n-1)/chi2(a/2, n-1))`.
- **CI of the median**: distribution-free, from order statistics, exact binomial and not interpolated. With the sample
  sorted ascending, `k` is the largest rank with `P(Binomial(n, 1/2) <= k-1) <= a/2` and the interval is
  `[x(k), x(n+1-k)]`; both ends are sample values. Its real coverage `1 - 2 P(Binomial(n,1/2) <= k-1)` is at least the
  level (conservative, so it is often wider than asked). When even `k = 1` cannot reach the level (n too small: n = 4
  at 95 %) the interval is `[min, max]` and a note gives its real confidence. Minitab-style interpolation between
  neighbouring order statistics is not used.
- **Normality Test**: Anderson-Darling A-squared and its p-value exactly as `calc::anderson_darling` gives them (the
  Normality Test tool; needs 8 numbers with spread, otherwise the cells are empty and a note says so).
- **Report sheet** `GraphSummary1` (Sheaf's layout): the first seven columns are long-form tables, text columns
  Variable, Group, Table, Statistic and number columns Value, Lower, Upper. The Table column holds "Description
  Statistics" (13 rows: N ... Maximum), "<level>% Confidence Intervals" (Mean, Median, Standard Deviation with estimate
  in Value and the limits in Lower and Upper) and "Normality Test" (A-Squared, P-Value). `Report.table` has one row per
  variable-group with the same numbers as columns (also the limits and A-Squared, P-Value).
- **Graph data** is written to the same sheet behind those seven columns, per variable-group: the histogram's columns
  (the Frequency Counts tool, automatic bins), the normal curve (101 points over the bin range,
  `N * bin width * pdf` of the sample mean and SD, so it is on the count axis), the two interval segments and the two
  estimates with their labels, and the Probability Plot tool's columns (normal, T14).
- **Graph** per variable-group, long name `<variable> <group>` (the variable alone without groups), page 11 x 7 in
  with three layers (Sheaf's layout, not Origin's): top left the histogram (bars) with the red normal curve, bottom
  left the interval plot (the mean and the median each a horizontal bar from lower to upper limit with a square at the
  estimate and its name; bars are line plots because the median interval is asymmetric and X error bars are
  symmetric), right the normal probability plot with the fitted line on a probability axis. The graph title is a text
  label above the histogram. Graphs are numbered in the order variable, then group: `new_graph` is the first, the rest
  are `more_graphs`.
- **Dialog**: input columns from the selection; Groups (a drop-down of check boxes over the other columns of the
  sheet), Confidence Level (%), Graph Arrangement. No X range (the tool takes row ranges only). Auto Apply is off for
  it (each run makes sheets and graphs); no Recalculate (the tool is not linkable).

## 5 Where results go (ADR 0005)

Every run is one `Project::analyze` call and one undo step named after the test ("Two-sample t-test",
"One-way ANOVA", "Histogram", "Normality test", "Two-way ANOVA", "Mann-Whitney test", "One-sample Wilcoxon signed
rank test", "Paired Wilcoxon signed rank test", "Kruskal-Wallis test", "Correlation coefficients"; section 16 adds "Distribution fit", "Probability plot" and "Q-Q plot"). Results go to a new
sheet of the workbook of the input (`TTest1`, `ANOVA1`, `Histogram1`, `Normality1`, `ANOVA2Way1`, `MannWhitney1`,
`SignedRank1`, `KruskalWallis1`, `Correlation1`); the histogram graph is a new graph window. The input
columns must be in one sheet and are the Y columns chosen (for the two-sample and paired test exactly
two, for ANOVA at least two; the two-way ANOVA takes three columns, factor A, factor B and data, which may be text
columns, or any number of data columns with levels). The app's window for the two-way ANOVA lists every column of
the sheet in its factor and data pickers, so text columns can be chosen even though the app passes only numeric
columns on to analysis windows. The chosen row range applies to every column. Column names in the
sentences and tables are the long name when set, otherwise the short name.

## 6 What was verified, and what was not

Verified against Origin runs: the t statistics, degrees of freedom, standard errors and p-values for
all three tests with several tails and levels; the ANOVA table, descriptive statistics and all five means
comparisons (p-values and interval ends); the five normality tests (see the table); frequency counts

chose. P-values from Origin's t and F distributions are off from the exact ones by up to 2e-5 (relative)

Verified against Origin runs in sections 7 to 9 (78 runs of the X-Functions, 23 runs of the
two-way library functions and one four-group one-way run, on inputs we generated, with ties, unequal group sizes, an unbalanced design,
constant data, groups and cells of one, and missing values): the two-way tables of balanced and unbalanced designs
