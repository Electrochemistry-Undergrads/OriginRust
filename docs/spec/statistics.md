## 1 t-tests

All three tests compute `t = (estimate - hypothesized value) / standard error`.

| Test | Estimate | Standard error | Degrees of freedom |
|---|---|---|---|
| One sample | mean of the column | `SD / sqrt(n)` | `n - 1` |
| Two sample, equal variance (Origin's default) | `mean1 - mean2` | pooled: `sqrt(s_p^2 (1/n1 + 1/n2))` | `n1 + n2 - 2` |
| Two sample, unequal variance (Welch) | `mean1 - mean2` | `sqrt(s1^2/n1 + s2^2/n2)` | Satterthwaite |
| Paired | mean of the differences of the rows where both columns have a number | `SD(d) / sqrt(n)` | `n - 1` |

Options and defaults in Origin: the hypothesized mean (or difference of means) is 0, the
significance level is 0.05, and the alternative is two-tailed. The upper tail alternative is "the
estimate is larger than the hypothesized value" (`p = P(T > t)`), the lower tail "smaller" (`p = P(T < t)`),
the two-sided p-value is `2 P(T > |t|)`. The null hypothesis is rejected when `p < alpha`.

Confidence interval: it belongs to the estimate (the mean, the difference of the means, the mean
difference), not to the test statistic. A two-tailed test gives the interval of level `1 - alpha`; a
one-tailed test gives the interval of level `1 - 2 alpha`, so that its one finite end is the one-sided
bound of level `1 - alpha` (for alpha 0.05 and an upper-tail test the 90 % interval is reported).
Origin's interval for the unequal-variance test is the pooled one (pooled standard error, `n1 + n2 - 2`
degrees of freedom), while its t, DF and Prob are Welch's. Sheaf does the same (measured: on
A = 10 + 2 sin(3i) + i/4, i = 1..8, and B = 11.5 + 2.5 cos(2i) + i/5, i = 1..10, Origin gives DF 15.634 and
LCL -3.34117967, UCL 0.17642427, the pooled interval to 8 digits; test
`welch_test_reports_the_pooled_interval_as_origin` in `crates/calc/src/hypothesis.rs`).

Sheaf reports N, mean, SD and SEM of each sample, the estimate, its standard error, the hypothesized
value, t, DF, Prob, the confidence level, LCL, UCL and a 0/1 Reject column, and one plain-English line
per test.

## 2 One-way ANOVA

Each column is a group (missing values are ignored). The summary table has the rows Model (between
groups), Error (within) and Total with DF, sum of squares and mean square, and for Model the
statistic F = MS(model) / MS(error) and `Prob>F` from the F distribution. Origin also reports the
descriptive statistics of the groups (N, mean, SD, SEM) and R-squared (`SS model / SS total`), the root of
the error mean square and its ratio to the grand mean ("COV"). Sheaf writes the same into one sheet:
a groups table, the ANOVA table, a small table of R-squared, root MSE, COV and grand mean, and the
comparison table below.

Means comparison of all pairs (Tukey is Origin's usual choice and our default). For groups `i < j`:
difference `mean_j - mean_i`, standard error `sqrt(MSE (1/n_i + 1/n_j))`, with `m` the number of pairs and
`t` the Student t with the error degrees of freedom:

| Method | Statistic | p-value | Interval half width |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tukey | q = `|diff| sqrt(2) / se` (studentized range) | `P(Q > q)` for k groups | `q_crit se / sqrt 2` |
| Bonferroni | `|t|` | `min(1, m p_t)` | `t_(alpha/m) se` |
| Sidak | `|t|` | `1 - (1 - p_t)^m` | `t` at `1 - (1-alpha)^(1/m)` |
| Fisher LSD | `|t|` | `p_t`, no adjustment | `t_alpha se` |
| Scheffe | F = `diff^2 / (se^2 (k-1))` | F distribution, k-1 and error DF | `sqrt((k-1) F_crit) se` |

The pairs come in Origin's order, column by column: (1,2), (1,3), (2,3), (1,4), (2,4), (3,4), ... (a run with
four groups showed this; the first version listed them row by row, which is the same for three groups).
A pair is marked significant when its p-value is below alpha. The studentized range distribution
(`ptukey`, `qtukey`) is our own implementation of the published algorithm; statrs has none. Two groups
give the same p-value as the pooled two-sample t-test (checked).

## 3 Frequency counts and histogram

`freqcounts` counts how many values fall into each bin. What black-box runs showed:

- A bin holds its lower edge and not its upper edge: `[lo, hi)`. A value exactly on the last edge of the
  range is in no bin (so counts can sum to less than N). With the number of bins given and no end, the
  largest value is included.
- The number of bins is `(max - min) / size`, rounded to the nearest whole number (10/3 gives 3 bins,
  7/2 gives 4).
- Outlier rows (values below the first edge and at or above the last) are optional. In one case Origin
  moves the largest value from the row above into the row below when both rows are on; that looks like a
  fault in Origin and is not copied.
- Bin centre is the middle of the bin; bin end is the upper edge. The result columns are bin centre or
  end, count, cumulative count, and percent.
- Automatic range: when the range and size are left on automatic, the first edge is a multiple of the
  bin size below the smallest value and the last edge a multiple above the largest, with a margin of
  about a tenth of a bin: a data value that comes within that margin of an edge makes the range one bin
  wider on that side. The margin is not a clean threshold in Origin (a gap of 0.0802 bins did not widen
  the end for one sample, 0.0855 did for another); ours is 0.1 at the start and 0.08 at the end, which
  reproduces 98 % of 600 random starts and ends.
- Automatic bin size (measured 2026-11-13 on 600 generated cases of Origin's `RoundBinningRange(min, max, n)`,
  which depends on n, min and max only; `tools/origin-ref/stats-bins2.lines.txt`, fixture
  `crates/calc/tests/fixtures/bins_origin.rs`): the number of bins is `m = round(1.75 ln n)` (at least 2;
  steps at n = 5, 8, 14, 24, 42), the size is `range / (m - 0.19)` rounded to the nearest round number:
  one significant digit for n < 14 (sizes such as 0.9, 3, 9, 40000 occur), and 1, 2 or 5 times a power of
  ten from n = 14 (midpoints 1.5, 3.5, 7.5). Sheaf reproduces 593 of 600 sizes (the misses lie within
  rounding of a midpoint; the offset 0.19 is a fit, 0.2 gives 9 misses). The first and last edge follow
  from the size as described above, with 28 differences in 566 cases; in a further 34 cases Origin returns a
  last edge that is not a multiple of the size (for example -1.6212428 for data ending at -1.622), not copied.
  When it matters the user gives the bin size or number of bins, which agree exactly. For 40 values with a
  range of about 9.4 Origin chose 5 bins of 2; for 8 values between 9.9 and 13.4 bins of 0.9.

The histogram tool in Sheaf writes the counts to a sheet (bin centre as X, bin end, count, cumulative
count, percent) and, unless switched off, makes a graph with a column plot (`PlotKind::Column`) of the
counts against the bin centres, with the count axis starting at zero. The bars are drawn by the existing
column plot (width 0.7 of the smallest centre spacing), so there is a gap between the bars; Origin's
histogram has none. Closing that gap needs a change in `crates/plot` and is not done.

## 4 Normality tests

Origin offers Shapiro-Wilk (`swtest`), Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Lilliefors, Anderson-Darling and
D'Agostino-Pearson; Sheaf computes all five for each column and reports the statistic and p-value of each.

| Test | Needs | Statistic | Agreement with Origin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shapiro-Wilk | 3 to 5000 numbers | W | W to 1e-9, p (Royston's approximation) to 1e-6 |
| Kolmogorov-Smirnov | 2 numbers | D against a normal with the sample mean and SD | D exact; p is the exact distribution for given parameters. 70 further Origin runs (n = 5 to 100, `ks-probe.lines.txt`) show: Origin agrees with the exact p within 3 % wherever p < 0.5; above that its p is higher than the exact one (0.97 against 0.86 for n = 40) and is exactly 1 for a scaled statistic `(sqrt(n) + 0.12 + 0.11/sqrt(n)) D` below about 0.57. It is not a single function of that scaled statistic and none of the Kolmogorov series (1 to 100 terms, Stephens' or plain scaling) reproduced it, so the rule is not copied |
| Lilliefors | 5 numbers | same D | p below 0.1 to six digits (Dallal and Wilkinson); above 0.1 Origin interpolates in a table we do not have, ours is within 8 % (checked on 70 more runs, n = 5 to 100); capped at 0.2 as in Origin |
| Anderson-Darling | 8 numbers | A squared | A squared to 1e-9, p to 1e-6 |
| D'Agostino-Pearson | 8 numbers (kurtosis part 5) | K squared with 2 DF | z-scores, K squared and p to 1e-6 |

Normality is rejected when the p-value is below alpha (0.05 by default). Constant data and samples
that are too small give no value for a test (shown as "--" and named in the sentence).

## 7 Two-way ANOVA

Origin: Statistics > ANOVA > Two-Way ANOVA. It is an operation (no X-Function can be run from LabTalk), so
the reference runs call the library functions behind it, `stats_anova_two_way_compute_anova_table` and
`stats_anova_two_way_means_comparison` (`stats2-ref.c`, `stats2-anova2.lines.txt`). The library's own help says
the table comes from linear regressions on dummy variables, which is what lets cells have different sizes
(empty cells are not supported).

**Input.** Origin takes indexed data (a column with the level of factor A of each row, one for factor B and
one with the data) or raw data (every column is one cell of the design; the levels of A and B are set per
column). It needs at least 4 numbers and at least two levels in each factor; rows with a missing number are
dropped. Levels are numbered in the order they first appear (indexed) or in the order of the columns (raw).
Sheaf takes both: `Tool::TwoWayAnova` with `TwoWayLayout::Indexed` (columns A, B, data; the factor columns may
hold text or numbers) or `TwoWayLayout::Raw` (the levels per data column in the spec); command line
`--y A,B,DATA` or `--raw --y COLS --levels-a ... --levels-b ...`.

**Table.** Rows A, B, A*B (only with the interaction switched on, which Sheaf does by default), Model, Error
and Total, with DF, sum of squares, mean square, F and Prob>F for every row that has a test.

**Which sums of squares (measured).** The sum of squares of a term is the rise of the residual sum of squares
when the term is taken out of the full model, with every factor coded by sum-to-zero columns. For a balanced
design this is the textbook table. For unequal cell sizes it is the type III sum of squares, as the runs showed
on a 2 x 3 design with cells of 3, 2, 4, 1, 3 and 2 numbers: with the interaction Origin gave 9.7878 for
factor A and 13.6434 for factor B, which are the type III values (type I gives 13.1499 for A and type II 9.7495);
without the interaction the numbers are 9.7495 and 13.9395, where type II and type III coincide. The same holds
on a second design with 3 x 2 levels. The Model row is total minus error with the
rank of the model minus 1 degrees of freedom, the Error row is the residual of the full model (so without the
interaction the interaction's sum of squares goes into the error), the Total row has N - 1 degrees of freedom.
R-squared is `SS model / SS total` and the root MSE the root of the error mean square.

**Edge cases.** With one number per cell and the interaction on, the error has no degrees of freedom and no F or
p is shown (Origin shows nothing there either); Sheaf also lists no means comparisons. Constant data give zero
sums of squares and no F. An empty cell (a combination of levels with no data) with the interaction on:
Origin's library does not stop but its table is inconsistent (it counts the empty cell in the degrees of freedom),
so Sheaf refuses and says which cell is empty; without the interaction an empty cell is fine, as long as the
levels are connected through the data (otherwise Sheaf refuses, the effects cannot be told apart).

**Means comparisons.** Tukey, Bonferroni, Sidak, Fisher LSD and Scheffe, as in section 2, for the levels of A, for
the levels of B and, with the interaction, for every one of the A x B cells (A changes slowest, so
A1B1, A1B2, ...). The mean of a level is the plain mean of its observations, the difference is `mean(j) - mean(i)`,
the standard error `sqrt(MSE (1/n_i + 1/n_j))` with MSE and its degrees of freedom from the Error row (the
marginal means are not adjusted for the other factor, which only matters in unbalanced designs). The statistic of
the t methods keeps the sign of the difference, the Tukey q and the Scheffe F do not. Origin's library numbers
the methods 1 Tukey, 2 Bonferroni, 3 Sidak, 4 Fisher, 5 Scheffe, which differs from the order in its header comment.
Holm methods and Dunnett are not built. For constant data Origin's 0/0 turns into p = 0 and "significant" for
equal means; Sheaf says p = 1.

**Sheet.** `ANOVA2Way1`: one table of N, mean, SD and SEM for each level of A, each level of B, each cell (with the
interaction) and all the data; the ANOVA table; R-squared and root MSE; the comparison table. The descriptive
statistics of the cells that Origin's library returns are only right for data sorted by cell (our test data are
shuffled), so they were not used as a reference; Sheaf computes them from the cells directly.

## 8 Nonparametric tests

Origin: Statistics > Nonparametric Tests; X-Functions `mwtest`, `signrank1`, `signrank2`, `kwanova`. Their
reports have a table of descriptive statistics, a table of ranks and a table of test statistics; Sheaf puts the
numbers in one table row per test and in the result sheet. Missing values are ignored. Ranks: tied numbers get the
mean of the ranks they span (the "tie correction" of the variance below is then needed and was confirmed).

**Mann-Whitney** (`mwtest`; only the raw form, one column per sample, is taken; Origin also has indexed data).
`R1` is the rank sum of the first sample in the pooled ranks, `U = R1 - n1 (n1 + 1) / 2` (Origin prints the U of the
first sample, not the smaller one). Normal approximation: mean `n1 n2 / 2`, variance
`n1 n2 / 12 ((N + 1) - sum(t^3 - t) / (N (N - 1)))`, continuity correction 0.5. The printed Z is
`(U - mean + 0.5) / sd` for U below the mean and `- 0.5` above it. The asymptotic p-value of the lower tail
("F(x) < G(y)", the first sample tends to be smaller) is `Phi((U - mean + 0.5) / sd)`, of the upper tail
`1 - Phi((U - mean - 0.5) / sd)`, two-sided twice the smaller of the two, at most 1. With the "exact" option Origin
adds the exact p-value: the permutation distribution of the rank sum of the first sample, using the mean ranks
of the pooled data (so ties are handled exactly, not ignored): lower tail `P(R <= R1)`, upper `P(R >= R1)`,
two-sided twice the smaller, at most 1. The runs matched it to 1e-9, with ties and unequal sizes up to 60 and 65
numbers (the largest run; Origin showed no limit; the table of subset sums Sheaf builds is limited to 4e8 cells, beyond which Sheaf
reports the asymptotic p and says so). With the exact option Origin also prints a second number, the asymptotic
p-value, which for the upper tail uses the plain `1 - Phi(Z)` and not the corrected one of the run without the
option; the two-sided and lower values are the same in both runs. Sheaf copies this: with `exact` the upper
asymptotic p is `1 - Phi(Z)` of the printed Z (all upper-tail runs in `stats2_origin.rs` match to 1e-9). If all
numbers are equal Origin gives Z = 0 and p = 1 (all tails); a group of one number gives no result in Origin, Sheaf
computes the test.

**Wilcoxon signed rank** (`signrank1` against a hypothesized median, default 0, and `signrank2` for pairs). The
differences are `x - median` or `x - y` (rows with a missing number in either column are dropped), differences of
exactly zero are left out (the count of the rest is N), the absolute values are ranked with mean ranks and
`T+` is the sum of the ranks of the positive differences, which is the statistic Origin prints (for pairs, the
sum of the ranks of rows where the first column is larger). Mean `N (N + 1) / 4`, variance
`N (N + 1) (2N + 1) / 24 - sum(t^3 - t) / 48`. `Z = (T+ - mean + 0.5) / sd` for T+ below the mean and `- 0.5`
above it; the asymptotic p is `2 Phi(-|Z|)` (two-sided), `1 - Phi(Z)` (upper tail, "the first is larger") and
`Phi(Z)` (lower tail); the one-sided values use the same Z, unlike Mann-Whitney's. Origin always prints the exact
p-value next to the asymptotic one, when there are at most 80 non-zero differences (81 differences print the
asymptotic value alone, and five zero differences among 85 values bring it back, which is how the limit was found).
The exact p is computed over the sign patterns with the mean ranks, lower `P(T <= T+)`, upper `P(T >= T+)`,
two-sided twice the smaller, at most 1; the runs agree to 1e-9, including ties among the differences. If there is
nothing to rank, Origin prints T = 0, no Z and p = 1; Sheaf says so in a sentence. Origin's normal tail gives 0 beyond
about 8 standard deviations, ours is exact there (compared with an absolute 1e-12).

**Kruskal-Wallis** (`kwanova`; raw form, one column per group). `H = 12 / (N (N + 1)) sum(R_i^2 / n_i) - 3 (N + 1)`
divided by `1 - sum(t^3 - t) / (N^3 - N)`; chi-squared with k - 1 degrees of freedom. A group of one number is
allowed. If all numbers are equal H = 0 and p = 1. Origin has no exact option, and none is built.

Sheaf's tools: `Tool::MannWhitney` (tail, exact, alpha), `Tool::SignedRank` (one-sample with the median, or paired;
tail; whether to decide with the exact p; alpha) and `Tool::KruskalWallis`; sheets `MannWhitney1`, `SignedRank1`,
`KruskalWallis1`. Each gives a sentence with the statistic, p-value and decision.

## 9 Correlation coefficients

Origin: Statistics > Descriptive Statistics > Correlation Coefficient (`corrcoef`, at least two columns;
Pearson on by default, Spearman and Kendall optional; missing values pairwise (default) or listwise). The report
has, for each column, N, mean, SD, sum, minimum and maximum, and for each coefficient the matrix of r with the matrix
of p-values (significance) below it; separate worksheets (`pwks`, `swks`, `kwks`) hold the plain r matrices.

Confirmed on four columns with ties and several missing-value layouts (agreement to 1e-12 in r, 5e-5 in p):

| Coefficient | r | p-value (two-sided) |
|---|---|---|
| Pearson | the usual product-moment r | t test, `t = r sqrt((n - 2) / (1 - r^2))`, n - 2 degrees of freedom |
| Spearman | Pearson's r of the mean ranks (so ties are handled) | the same t test |
| Kendall | tau-b, `S / sqrt((n0 - n1) (n0 - n2))` with S = concordant minus discordant pairs | normal test of `S / sqrt(Var S)`, `Var S = (n (n - 1) (2n + 5) - sum t (t - 1) (2t + 5) - sum u (u - 1) (2u + 5)) / 18 + sum t (t - 1) sum u (u - 1) / (2 n (n - 1)) + sum t (t - 1) (t - 2) sum u (u - 1) (u - 2) / (9 n (n - 1) (n - 2))`, no continuity correction |

There is no p-value where r is exactly +1 or -1 (which includes every case of two pairs) and for the diagonal;
a constant column has no coefficients at all (its row and column are empty, including the diagonal). Pairwise
deletion uses, for each pair of columns, the rows where both have a number. With missing values Origin's
Kendall p-value differs from this formula by up to 17 % (re-examined 2026-11-13: for the four pairs with a missing row the variance Origin implies is 127.7, 165.0, 162.9 and 160.7 against 124.0, 163.0, 159.1 and 158.4 here; the exact permutation p, the full-column tie counts and tau-b scaling variants did not reproduce them, and with only four rounded values the rule was not found) in the pairs that lost rows (its tie bookkeeping is not
the textbook one and was not worked out); the coefficient itself agrees, and the Pearson and Spearman p-values
agree in all cases. Sheaf keeps the textbook formula and the test does not compare those Kendall p-values.

Sheaf's `Tool::Correlation` (coefficients chosen with flags, listwise or pairwise, alpha for the sentence that
names the significant pairs) writes `Correlation1`: the descriptive table, and for each coefficient the matrix of r
(variable names down the side, one column per variable), the matrix of p-values and, when pairs have different
counts, the matrix of N; the report table has one row per pair with N, r and p of every coefficient.
Kendall's score is counted in O(n log n) time (sort by one column, count the inversions of the other by a merge
sort, correct for ties), so hundreds of thousands of rows take well under a second.

Two-way ANOVA lists the pairwise comparison of the cells only when the design has at most 64 cells, because the
pairs grow with the square of the cells and Tukey's studentized range probabilities are costly. The factor
comparisons are always listed. Comparisons are left out (not a panic) when the data contain an infinite value.

## 10 Sign, two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov, median and Friedman tests; outlier tests; row statistics

Origin X-Functions `sign2`, `kstest2`, `mediantest`, `friedman`, `grubbs`, `qtest` and `rowstats`, run from
`tools/origin-ref/stats3/` (generators `g2.py` to `g5.py`, compact raw output `stats3-p*.compact.txt`, the
fixture `crates/calc/tests/fixtures/stats3_origin.rs` written by `stats3-make-fixture.py`, the test
`crates/calc/tests/origin_stats3_tests.rs`). About 200 runs on inputs we generated, with ties, missing values,
constant data and one or two numbers. Origin has no one-sample sign test X-Function (`sign2` is the paired
one); the other Nonparametric Tests entries of the index (`NPHKSamples`, `NPHPairSample`, `NPHTwoSample`) are
the dialog front ends and were not run. The report sheets have no header cells we could read through
automation, so the meaning of each number was inferred from the values (all inferences were then confirmed by
the 1e-9 agreement of the test).

**Paired sign test** (`sign2`; two columns, rows with a missing number in either are dropped; the `null`
argument is only a text). Pairs with equal numbers are left out: `N = S + T`, where `S` is the count of pairs
with the first number smaller (the statistic Origin prints) and `T` with the second smaller. At most 25 such
pairs: exact binomial p-value. With `m = min(S, T)` and `pm = P(Bin(N, 1/2) <= m)`: two-sided `min(1, 2 pm)`;
one-sided, "lower" is `pm` when `S < T` and `1 - pm` when `S > T`, "upper" the other way round, and both are 0.5
when `S = T` (so the one-sided values are not the textbook ones: the direction is chosen by which count is
larger). Origin's binomial tail is off by up to 1.4e-6 relative. From 26 pairs up: normal approximation with
continuity correction, `Z = (|S - N/2| - 1/2) / (sqrt(N) / 2)` (negative when `S = N/2`), two-sided
`min(1, 2 (1 - Phi(|Z|)))`, upper `1 - Phi(Z)`, lower `Phi(Z)` (here the direction does not matter: swapping the
columns gives the same one-sided values). Z is printed only then. All pairs equal: p = 1 for every tail. Fewer than
two pairs: no result. Tail names follow Origin's `F(x) < G(y)` / `F(x) > G(y)`; Sheaf's `Upper` is the latter.

**Two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov** (`kstest2`, raw form; it prints N1, N2, D, Z and p). `D+` is the largest
`F1 - F2`, `D-` the largest `F2 - F1` of the empirical distribution functions (evaluated at the distinct pooled
values), `D = max(D+, D-)`; the tail "upper" (`F(x) > G(y)`) uses `D+`, "lower" `D-`, two-sided `D`. `Z = sqrt(n1 n2 /
(n1 + n2)) D`. The two-sided p-value of a distance `D` is `P(D_{n1,n2} >= D)` of the continuous distribution
(lattice-path count, ties in the data are ignored even in the exact value: heavily tied samples matched to 1e-9)
when `n1 n2 <= 10000` (checked up to 100 x 100 exact and 60 x 200 not exact; the limit lies between 10000 and
12000), and above that `Q(Z + 0.5 / sqrt(max(n1, n2)))` with Kolmogorov's `Q(z) = 2 sum (-1)^(j-1) exp(-2 j^2 z^2)`
(fitted to six runs; the p-values agree to better than 1e-7 relative: 100 x 120, 300 x 400, 400 x 300, 250 x 50,
200 x 150, 120 x 100). A one-sided p-value is half of the two-sided p-value of that one-sided `D` (so 0.5 when
the distance is 0). Either sample with fewer than two numbers: no result.

**Median test** (`mediantest`, raw form). The overall median of all numbers is the ordinary median. Each group
is counted at or below the median and above it (a number equal to the median counts as below); `chi2 = sum
(O - E)^2 / E` over the `2 x k` table, no continuity correction and no Fisher test, `k - 1` degrees of freedom.
Cells with expected count 0 are skipped (all numbers equal: chi2 = 0, df = k - 1, p = 1). Groups of one number
are allowed; chi-square tail agrees to 2e-5 relative.

**Friedman ANOVA** (`friedman`, raw form: columns are treatments, rows blocks). Ranks within each row with mean
ranks for ties; `chi2 = 12 / (n k (k + 1)) sum R_j^2 - 3 n (k + 1)`, **no correction for ties** (the textbook
tie-corrected statistic differs from Origin's number; checked with ties in many rows), `k - 1` degrees of freedom,
chi-square p. All numbers equal: chi2 = 0, p = 1. The report also has N, minimum, quartiles, median, maximum, mean
rank and rank sum of each column. Origin writes **no report** when a row has a missing number or the columns
have different lengths, and Sheaf refuses too, with a sentence.

**Grubbs** (`grubbs`; inputs the data and a level 0.1, 0.05 or 0.01 offered, any level accepted). Missing values
are ignored but the index counts them as rows. `G = max |x - mean| / SD` of the point farthest from the mean (the
lower one when both ends are equally far); critical value `(n - 1) / sqrt(n) sqrt(t^2 / (n - 2 + t^2))` with `t`
the upper `alpha / (2 n)` quantile of Student's t with `n - 2` degrees of freedom; "significant" is `G >
critical`. The "approximate p-value" is `n * 2 * P(T > t_G)` with `t_G = G sqrt(n (n - 2) / ((n - 1)^2 - n G^2))`
and `n - 2` degrees of freedom; it is **not limited to 1** (5.06 was seen) and is not given when `G` has its
largest possible value `(n - 1) / sqrt(n)`. At least three numbers with a non-zero SD; two numbers or constant
data give no result. Origin's t quantile is off by up to 5e-6 relative, so critical values and p-values agree to
2e-5. The sentences are "At the 0.05 level, the highest (lowest) value X is a significant outlier" and "... there
is no significant outlier".

**Dixon's Q test** (`qtest`; levels 0.1, 0.05 and 0.01 only, 3 to 10 numbers; two numbers or more than ten give
no result, constant data: "All valid input data are equal"). `Q = gap / range` for the lowest value (gap to the
second lowest) and the highest value (gap to the second highest); the larger is tested, the lowest on a tie;
"significant" is `Q > critical`. Critical values (n = 3 to 10) at 0.1, 0.05, 0.01: 0.941, 0.970, 0.994; 0.765,
0.829, 0.926; 0.642, 0.710, 0.821; 0.560, 0.625, 0.740; 0.507, 0.568, 0.680; 0.468, 0.526, 0.634; 0.437, 0.493,
0.598; 0.412, 0.466, 0.568 (all 24 measured, the table is in `crates/calc/src/outlier.rs`).

**Statistics on Rows** (`rowstats`; outputs mean and SD by default, standard error, minimum, maximum, number of
points and sum optional). One number per worksheet row across the chosen columns; NaN ignored. A row with no
number gives missing values in every output including the count and the sum; one number has no SD and no SE.
Sheaf adds the chosen columns behind the last chosen column (`Tool::RowStatistics`, long names "Mean", "Standard
Deviation", "SE of Mean", "Minimum", "Maximum", "Number of Points", "Sum"). Origin's own dialog has more options
(for example row ranges as input) that were not measured.

Sheaf: `Tool::SignTest`, `Tool::KsTwoSample`, `Tool::MedianTest`, `Tool::Friedman`, `Tool::Grubbs`, `Tool::Dixon`
and `Tool::RowStatistics`; result sheets `SignTest1`, `KolmogorovSmirnov1`, `MedianTest1`, `Friedman1`,
`Grubbs1`, `Dixon1`; command line `analyze sign | ks2 | mediantest | friedman | grubbs | dixon | rowstats`; menu
entries Statistics > Nonparametric Tests > Two Sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov Test, Mood's Median Test, Friedman ANOVA,
Paired Sample Sign Test and Statistics > Descriptive Statistics > Statistics on Rows, Grubbs Test, Dixon's Q-test
(labels from the X-Function descriptions). Not done: the indexed input forms, the outlier box plot (`box`),
Origin's report layout, outlier tests that remove several points in turn.

## 12 Distribution fit, probability plot and Q-Q plot, power and sample size

(Section number chosen on the `stats-dist` branch; the `stats-compare` branch adds section 11, so this one comes
after it and may be renumbered at merge.)

**Source of the numbers: scipy, not Origin.** Nothing in this section was measured against Origin (this was
built on a machine without it). The reference numbers are from scipy 1.18 (`scipy.stats.<dist>.fit`, `probplot`,
`kstest`, `nct`, `ncf`, `t`, `f`), written by `tools/stats-dist-ref.py` into
`crates/calc/tests/fixtures/scipy_stats_dist.rs` and checked by `crates/calc/tests/scipy_stats_dist.rs`. Every
rule below is the textbook one; where Origin may differ the item is listed under "Not measured".

The numerics are in `crates/calc` (`distfit`, `noncentral`, `power`), the tools in
`crates/core/src/analysis/dist.rs`, the command line is `sheaf analyze distfit | probplot | qqplot | power`, and
the app has Statistics > Descriptive Statistics > Distribution Fit, Probability Plot, Q-Q Plot and the
Statistics > Power and Sample Size submenu (one window per test; options in `dialogs/dist_options.rs`).

### 12.1 Distribution fit

Five families, each fitted to each chosen column by maximum likelihood (missing values are ignored; at least two
numbers with some spread). Lognormal, exponential, gamma and Weibull have their threshold fixed at 0, as
`scipy.stats.<dist>.fit(x, floc=0)` does, so they need positive numbers (the exponential accepts 0). A family that
cannot take a column is left out for that column with a note; if none can, the run is refused.

| Family | Parameters | Estimate |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | mean, SD | sample mean; SD with divisor n |
| Lognormal | mu, sigma of the logarithm | mean and SD (divisor n) of ln x |
| Exponential | scale (the mean) | sample mean |
| Gamma | shape k, scale | k solves `ln k - digamma(k) = ln(mean) - mean(ln x)` (Newton), scale = mean / k |
| Weibull | shape c, scale | c solves the likelihood equation (bisection on ln c), scale from c |

Reported per row: N, the parameters, log-likelihood `ln L`, `AIC = 2k - 2 ln L`, `BIC = k ln n - 2 ln L` (k = number
of fitted parameters, the fixed threshold not counted), Kolmogorov-Smirnov D and p, Anderson-Darling A2 (and an
approximate p for normal and lognormal). The notes name the family with the lowest AIC. KS p is for a distribution
known in advance (as `scipy.stats.kstest`), so it is too large when the parameters were estimated from the same
data; this is said in the note. A2 is `-n - (1/n) sum (2i-1) (ln F(x_i) + ln(1 - F(x_(n+1-i))))` against the fitted
distribution. The A2 p-value uses the D'Agostino-Stephens formula for estimated mean and SD, applied to the
normal and to the lognormal (on the logarithm); no p is given for the other families.

Result sheet `DistFit1`: columns Data, Distribution, N, Parameter 1 (name), Value 1, Parameter 2 (name),
Value 2 (empty for the exponential), Log-likelihood, AIC, BIC, KS D, KS Prob, Anderson-Darling A2 and Prob.

### 12.2 Probability plot and Q-Q plot

Both use the plotting positions of `scipy.stats.probplot` (Filliben's medians of the uniform order statistics:
`(i - 0.3175) / (n + 0.365)`, with `1 - 0.5^(1/n)` and `0.5^(1/n)` at the ends).

- **Q-Q plot** (`QQPlot1`): X is the theoretical quantile at the plotting position, Y the ordered data; the
  reference line is the least-squares line through the points (slope estimates the scale, intercept the location,
  `r` printed). Families with a shape (lognormal sigma, gamma, Weibull) take the shape fitted by maximum
  likelihood; the quantiles are those of the standard form, as `probplot(..., sparams=shape)`. A linear graph.
- **Probability plot** (`ProbPlot1`): X is the ordered data, Y the plotting position in percent; the Y axis is a
  probability axis (normal and lognormal use the normal probability scale, Weibull and exponential the Weibull
  scale; gamma the normal one), the X axis is logarithmic for lognormal, Weibull and (positive data) exponential.
  The reference line is the distribution function of the maximum likelihood fit over the range of the data, kept
  to 0.01 % to 99.99 %.

Both make a new graph (a scatter plot and a line plot per column, the line in the colour of its points) unless
"Make a graph" is off, plus the result sheet with the plotted columns and a table (N, parameters or slope,
intercept, r). One undo step removes sheet and graph.

### 12.3 Power and sample size

Equal variances, balanced designs, textbook formulas. With `d` = difference over standard deviation (the SD of the
differences for pairs):

| Test | df | Non-centrality |
|---|---|---|
| One sample, paired (n pairs) | n - 1 | `d sqrt(n)` |
| Two sample, n1 and n2 = ceil(ratio n1) | n1 + n2 - 2 | `d / sqrt(1/n1 + 1/n2)` |
| One-way ANOVA, k groups, n each, N = kn | k - 1, N - k | `f^2 N` |

The t statistic then has a noncentral t distribution: two-tailed power = `P(T > c) + P(T < -c)` with `c` the
`1 - alpha/2` quantile of the central t; one-tailed `P(T > c)` with the `1 - alpha` quantile (the effect is taken
on the side of the alternative, so upper and lower give the same number). The ANOVA power is `P(F' > F_crit)` with
a noncentral F. Cohen's `f` is the SD of the group means about their mean (divisor k) over the common SD; the window
takes `f` or the means and the SD. The sample size is the smallest integer whose power reaches the wanted power
(doubling and bisection; refused above 10 million). The one-sample test's sample size counts observations, the paired
test's pairs, the two-sample test's the first sample (the second follows from the ratio), ANOVA's per group.
Needs no data: any column of the active sheet only names the workbook that receives the sheet `Power1`.

Noncentral t: Lenth's AS 243 series in the incomplete beta function; above |ncp| 37.62 a normal approximation (the
answer is 1 to 1e-10 there). Noncentral F: the Poisson mixture of incomplete beta functions summed outward from
the largest weight.

### 12.4 Checked against scipy, tolerances

Normal, lognormal and exponential estimates, log-likelihood, AIC, BIC, KS D, A2, probability plot quantiles, slope,
intercept and r: 1e-9 relative. Gamma shape: 1e-9 against `scipy.stats.gamma.fit`. Weibull: scipy's general
optimizer stops 1e-5 to 2.5e-5 short of the maximum, so against its fit the parameters agree to 1e-4; against the
same equation solved with `scipy.optimize.brentq` to machine precision they agree to 1e-9, and the quantities
computed from the parameters are compared with scipy's for those parameters. KS p: 1e-7 (Marsaglia-Tsang-Wang
against scipy's exact algorithm). `nct` and `ncf` cdf and sf, power and sample size: 1e-9 absolute plus relative.

## 5 Where results go (ADR 0005)

Every run is one `Project::analyze` call and one undo step named after the test ("Two-sample t-test",
"One-way ANOVA", "Histogram", "Normality test", "Two-way ANOVA", "Mann-Whitney test", "One-sample Wilcoxon signed
rank test", "Paired Wilcoxon signed rank test", "Kruskal-Wallis test", "Correlation coefficients"; section 12 adds "Distribution fit", "Probability plot", "Q-Q plot" and "Power and sample size of the ... test"). Results go to a new
sheet of the workbook of the input (`TTest1`, `ANOVA1`, `Histogram1`, `Normality1`, `ANOVA2Way1`, `MannWhitney1`,
`SignedRank1`, `KruskalWallis1`, `Correlation1`); the histogram graph is a new graph window. The input
columns must be in one sheet and are the Y columns chosen (for the two-sample and paired test exactly
two, for ANOVA at least two; the two-way ANOVA takes three columns, factor A, factor B and data, which may be text
columns, or any number of data columns with levels). The app's window for the two-way ANOVA lists every column of
the sheet in its factor and data pickers, so text columns can be chosen even though the app passes only numeric
columns on to analysis windows. The chosen row range applies to every column. Column names in the
sentences and tables are the long name when set, otherwise the short name.

## 6 What was verified, and what was not

Verified against Origin runs: the t statistics, degrees of freedom, standard errors and p-values for
all three tests with several tails and levels; the ANOVA table, descriptive statistics and all five means
comparisons (p-values and interval ends); the five normality tests (see the table); frequency counts

chose. P-values from Origin's t and F distributions are off from the exact ones by up to 2e-5 (relative)

Verified against Origin runs in sections 7 to 9 (78 runs of the X-Functions, 23 runs of the
two-way library functions and one four-group one-way run, on inputs we generated, with ties, unequal group sizes, an unbalanced design,
constant data, groups and cells of one, and missing values): the two-way tables of balanced and unbalanced designs

each factor and for the cells, the type III rule against the type I and II numbers, the one-way comparison order
for four groups; the Mann-Whitney U, Z, rank sums and asymptotic and exact p-values for all tails; the signed
rank statistic, Z and both p-values for one sample and pairs, with ties and zero differences, and the limit of
80 for the exact value; the Kruskal-Wallis mean ranks, H and p; the three correlation matrices of r and of p.

Not verified or not done:

- The automatic bin size is a rule fitted to 600 measured cases (593 agree).
- Origin's own sheet layout and graph (report sheets with several tables, histogram without gaps,
  distribution curve on the histogram, normal probability plot) are not copied.
- No test of equal variance (Levene, F test) next to the two-sample t-test, no t-test from summary data, no row-wise t-tests.

  ANOVA, partial correlation, scatter matrices and confidence ellipses, outlier tests other than Grubbs and

  data column) is not offered. The correlation matrices of Origin's pwks/swks/kwks sheets are inside Sheaf's one
  result sheet, not separate sheets.
- Origin's Kendall p-values with missing values, its empty-cell two-way table and its cell statistics for
  unsorted data differ from ours (see the sections) and were not copied.
- Several of the new Origin numbers were produced with a normal tail that returned 0 at 8.5 standard
  deviations; those are compared with an absolute tolerance.
- The app window was checked with automated click tests, not looked at by the owner.
